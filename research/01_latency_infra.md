## Executive summary

All numbers marked "MEASURED" below are my own direct on-chain measurements taken 2026-08-23 against api.mainnet-beta.solana.com around slot 441,149,399, not repeated from marketing pages. The single most important finding: pump.fun's fee market is a *local* fee market that is 100-200x the global one. Global non-zero prioritization floor was ~2,986 microlamports/CU, while the pump.fun bonding-curve program's local floor ran p50=100,002 and p75=535,714 microlamports/CU in the same 150-slot window. Sampling 272 successful pump.fun transactions from 14 full blocks, landed cu_price was p50=264,166, p75=812,534, p90=4,000,000 and p99=40,000,000 microlamports/CU — so a competitive snipe needs roughly 1-4 million microlamports/CU, and the p50 bot is paying ~0.26 SOL-cents of priority fee, not the "medium 40,000 microlamports" that generic fee APIs return. Jito tips are a separate and thinner channel: only 12% of landed pump.fun txs carried a Jito tip at all, and among those the distribution was bimodal — p50=10,000 lamports but p90=3,000,000 and p99=5,000,000 lamports (0.003-0.005 SOL). Jito's own tip_floor API read 75th percentile = 9,523 lamports at the same moment, which is a network-wide floor and is badly misleading as a snipe target; budget 0.001-0.005 SOL for a contested mint, not 0.00001. Compute budgets: measured whole-transaction CU consumed was pump.fun bonding curve median 153,860 (min 55,587), PumpSwap AMM median 85,938, Raydium AMM v4 median 142,828, Raydium CPMM median 25,304. Set 100k CU for a warm-ATA PumpSwap buy and ~150k for a pump.fun buy including ATA creation; the sol-trade-sdk production default is exactly 150,000 CU limit / 500,000 microlamports price, which matches my measured local fee market closely. A critical and underreported finding: 77.1% of transactions touching the pump.fun bonding curve program FAIL (n=4,000), but inspecting the failures shows the dominant mode is *not* slippage — it is third-party sniper "guard" programs (e.g. 4qjYbRdexhF9hXDfzYScnzEAzE6GnUjB1RKjhEakqyu9, 7JwTi9ambvCTYwZbNQEZA8hzpG3ymPWRZpAxgKPJ94ZG) aborting with custom error 0x7 after consuming only ~950-2,750 CU. Professional snipers deliberately front their swap with a cheap on-chain precondition check so a lost race costs ~5,000 lamports base fee instead of a bad fill. You should copy this pattern. On colocation: I pulled the live leader schedule — 676 validators, 432,000 slots/epoch, strictly 4 consecutive slots per leader, top-10 leaders = 24.48% of slots, and just 40 of 676 validators produce 50% of all slots. That concentration is why multi-region submission beats single-region: you cannot colocate next to 40 validators, so you fan out. Latency ordering is settled and consistent across sources: raw shreds (sub-50ms, 100-500ms ahead of Geyser) > Geyser/LaserStream gRPC (p50 ~3ms delivery delta, ~8ms faster than `processed`) > websocket logsSubscribe > polling (2-5s). Use Geyser transactionSubscribe with a program filter as the baseline detector and shreds only if you are willing to pay $800-1,000/month/IP and handle deshredding yourself. On MEV: pump.fun's bonding curve is a constant-product curve and is fully sandwichable. Sandwich bots extracted $370-500M over Jan 2024-May 2025; the Vpe bot alone did 1.55M sandwiches in 30 days at an average of 0.0425 SOL ($8.67) profit per attack, and 16 of the top 20 sandwiched tokens were pump.fun launches. Your slippage setting is the attack surface: Jupiter's published dynamic-slippage config caps `pump_new_graduate_first_hour` at 1000-2000 bps and `degen_buy` at 1000-2500 bps, and those caps are the ceiling a sandwicher can extract. Mitigations in order of effectiveness: Jito bundles (atomic, unsandwichable within the bundle), Helius Sender with `?mev-protect=true` (routes around validators statistically linked to sandwiching), hard slippage caps, and never using a public mempool-visible path. Where sources disagree I have flagged it — notably Helius Sender's minimum tip is documented as 0.001 SOL on the current docs page but 0.0002 SOL in the zero-slot blog and in the sol-trade-sdk constant; treat 0.001 SOL as current.

## Traps & failure modes

- Calling getPriorityFeeEstimate or getRecentPrioritizationFees WITHOUT account keys returns the global market (~2,986 microlamports/CU measured) when the pump.fun local market is 100,002-535,714. This single mistake will make you lose essentially every contested race while believing your fees are correct.
- Using Jito's tip_floor p75 (9,523 lamports measured live) as your tip target. It aggregates all network bundles including arbitrage spam and is 100-500x below what landed competitive pump.fun transactions actually pay (p90 = 3,000,000 lamports). Build your own program-scoped tip percentile tracker.
- Priority fee is charged on the REQUESTED compute unit limit, not the consumed amount. Observed bots request 1,400,000 CU on pump.fun buys that consume 153,860. At 500,000 microlamports/CU that wastes 0.000623 SOL per attempt. Over 1,000 attempts/day that is 0.62 SOL/day burned for nothing.
- Interpreting the 77.1% pump.fun failure rate as slippage failures. It is not — it is competitors' guard programs aborting cheaply at ~1-3k CU. If you naively raise slippage to 'fix' failures you convert cheap losses into expensive bad fills and hand the difference to sandwichers.
- Setting a wide percentage slippage on a buy. Your slippage cap IS the sandwicher's maximum extraction budget. A 2500 bps degen_buy setting is an explicit offer to lose 25%. Compute an absolute minimum-out from observed reserves instead.
- Broadcasting buys through a public/shared RPC sendTransaction. Solana has no public mempool, so sandwiching requires transaction visibility before execution — a leaky shared RPC or a colluding validator is exactly that leak. Buys must go through Jito bundles, Helius Sender with mev-protect=true, or a paid private relay.
- Using a fixed Jito tip account. All 8 are hardcoded and widely used; picking one creates an account write-lock hotspot that serializes your transaction against every other bot using the same account. Randomize per transaction.
- Jito's default rate limit is 1 request/second/IP/region. A bot that assumes it can spam sendBundle will get HTTP 429s during exactly the launch windows it cares about. Either fan across regions, use multiple IPs, or route through Helius Sender (50 TPS).
- Using blockSubscribe for detection. It has documented stability problems on high-traffic programs — connection errors and silently skipped data. You will miss launches and not know it. Use Geyser transactionSubscribe with a program filter.
- Sequential submit-then-fallback instead of concurrent fan-out. Each sequential hop costs a full RTT (~1 slot). By the time your second attempt goes out the mint is 400-800ms old and already sniped.
- Shred-derived signals are PRE-EXECUTION. A transaction visible in shreds may still fail or be reordered. Acting on a shred signal without re-validating against confirmed state means you will sometimes buy into a launch that never actually happened.
- Simulating the transaction before a snipe to size the CU limit. simulateTransaction costs a full network RTT you cannot afford in a race. Precompute per-venue CU constants offline (the measured distributions above are tight enough) and add a fixed margin.
- Provider documentation drift. sol-trade-sdk's minimum-tip constants are already stale for at least two providers (Nozomi listed as 0.0001 when its docs say 0.001; Helius listed as 0.0002 when its docs say 0.001). Re-verify every minimum against live provider docs before production or your transactions will be silently deprioritized.
- Single-provider dependency for detection. Provider incidents happen, and an outage during a launch window is a total loss of that opportunity with no partial credit. Run two independent detection feeds with signature dedup.
- Vendor-published benchmarks (BlockRazor's own study naming BlockRazor the winner; shreder.xyz benchmarking competitor Helius) are marketing. Even BlockRazor's self-favorable numbers show only 30-40% first-arrival. Instrument your own per-provider, per-region win rate and reweight from that.
- Assuming Helius LaserStream is or is not Yellowstone-compatible based on secondary sources — competitor pages claim it uses a proprietary wire format while Helius's own docs call it a drop-in replacement. This determines whether your existing yellowstone-grpc client code works. Verify against Helius docs directly before architecting around it.
- Paying for Raw Shreds ($800-1,000/mo/IP) before the rest of the pipeline is sub-50ms. The 100-500ms shred advantage is invisible if you then spend 200ms on transaction construction or a cold TLS handshake. Optimize the pipeline first, buy shreds last.
- Not accounting for the 4-slot leader run. If you miss the current leader you have ~1.6 seconds until the next one — enough time for a region-switched retry. Bots that blindly resubmit to the same endpoint waste that entire window.

### Real landed pump.fun transactions pay p50=264k and p90=4M microlamports/CU; only 12% carry a Jito tip and the tip distribution is bimodal

Results — cu_price microlamports/CU: p50=264,166, p75=812,534, p90=4,000,000, p99=40,000,000, max=116,999,610. **Actionable:** Tier your spend by conviction. Tier 1 (routine entry, non-contested): cu_price 500,000, CU limit 150,000, no Jito tip -> ~0.000080 SOL priority + 0.000005 base. Gate Tier 3 behind an expected-value check: at 0.005 SOL/attempt you need >0.005 SOL expected profit, which at typical memecoin hit rates means position sizes of at least 0.5-1 SOL.

### Jito's tip_floor API reports a network-wide floor that is 100-500x below what a competitive snipe actually needs — do not use it as your tip target

MEASURED live 2026-08-23T11:35:10Z from https://bundles.jito.wtf/api/v1/bundles/tip_floor (raw response): landed_tips_25th_percentile=1.0e-6 SOL, 50th=4.5355e-6, 75th=9.5235e-6, 95th=0.0001, 99th=0.0001 SOL, ema_landed_tips_50th_percentile=5.1567e-6 SOL. That p75 of 9,523 lamports is two to three orders of magnitude below my measured p90 pump.fun tip of 3,000,000 lamports. The API aggregates ALL bundles network-wide (including cheap arbitrage spam), not the contested-mint subset. Note the 95th and 99th percentiles both pinning at exactly 0.0001 suggests a reporting cap or rounding artifact in the endpoint. A WebSocket stream of the same data is at wss://bundles.jito.wtf/api/v1/bundles/tip_stream. Jito's documented absolute minimum tip is 1,000 lamports. Jito takes a 5% fee on tips per their own MEV report (one secondary source says 6% — Jito's own figure of 5% is more reliable).

**Actionable:** Poll tip_floor (or subscribe to tip_stream) for a *floor sanity check only*, then apply a multiplier derived from your own observed competition. Concretely: tip = max(tip_floor_p75 * 50, 1_000_000 lamports) for a contested mint. Build your own percentile tracker instead: stream landed blocks, sum deltas to the 8 Jito tip accounts for transactions touching your target program, and maintain a rolling p75/p90 of *that* subset. This is the highest-value piece of custom telemetry you can build.

Raydium LaunchLab (LanMV9sAd7wArD4vJFi2qDdfnVhFxYSUg6eADduJ3uj): 37.6% success / 62.4% fail, Custom:7 = 53.2%. ### Sandwich attacks cost memecoin buyers $370-500M over 16 months and disproportionately target pump.fun tokens; average extraction is 0.0425 SOL per attack

The single Vpe bot generated $13.43M over 30 days (Dec 7 - Jan 5) executing 1.55 million sandwich transactions, averaging 0.0425 SOL ($8.67) profit per attack, annualizing to a projected 801,540 SOL. A January 2025 analytics review found sandwich attacks in 112 of 150 DeFi tokens traded that week; of the top 20 most-sandwiched tokens, 16 were pump.fun creations. Majority of victim transactions were Raydium swaps. Sandwich bots pay only 5-15% of extracted value out as tips and keep the rest — meaning they can outbid you on tips by a wide margin while remaining profitable. Solana has no public mempool, so sandwiching requires the attacker to see your transaction before execution: this happens via a colluding/leaking validator, a shared RPC that leaks, or shred observation. Per the Helius MEV report, the Agave 1.18 scheduler reduced reverted transactions from a peak of 75.7% of non-vote transactions in April 2024.

**Actionable:** Layered mitigation, implement all: (1) Send buys ONLY through private paths — Jito bundles, Helius Sender with mev-protect=true, or a paid relay. Never broadcast a buy via a generic public RPC sendTransaction. (2) Use Jito bundles for the buy specifically: within a bundle your transaction cannot be sandwiched by another bundle because execution is atomic and sequential. (3) Compute minimum-out from the reserves you observed and enforce it on-chain, do not use a naive percentage. (4) Because 0.0425 SOL is the average extraction and your typical position may be 0.5-2 SOL, sandwiching is a 2-8% tax — that is larger than your tip budget, so paying 0.003 SOL for a protected path is clearly positive EV. (5) Vary your tip account and submission path so you are not fingerprintable as a predictable victim.
