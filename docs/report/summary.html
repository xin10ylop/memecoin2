The base rate

The median new token never goes up

Not "goes up a little" — never trades above its first observed price at all. This is measured, not assumed: the collector records every new mint within seconds of its first pool and follows it forward, so the census includes the overwhelming majority that die quietly.

Where the money is

Mean peak multiple across all 3,298 launches is 5.26×. Excluding just the top 1% of tokens it is 1.25×. Essentially the entire expected value of the asset class lives in a handful of tokens, which is why the exit rule matters more than the entry rule — it decides whether the one trade in three hundred pays for the other 299.

The independent one

Initial market cap is the strongest single gate and it is nearly uncorrelated with liquidity (r = 0.06), so it is genuinely separate evidence rather than a restatement. The 8.7% base 2× rate becomes 14.7% above $3k, 24.3% above $5k, and 35.1% above $7.5k. This came from external research and replicated cleanly on our own census.

The hurdle

What a trade has to clear before it earns a cent

The pump.fun bonding curve charges 1.25% per side — 0.95% protocol plus a 0.30% creator fee, not the 1% usually quoted. Fixed transaction costs turn out to be almost irrelevant; the proportional costs are the whole game.

What the stop is worth

Cutting the loss at −45% instead of riding positions to zero roughly halves the hit rate you need. That is the quantitative case for the hard stop, and it is larger than any plausible improvement to the entry rule.

The exit

Banking a runner that round-trips

The classic way to lose on a good pick is to ride a 10× back to break-even because no rule ever said to sell. The policy recovers cost basis early, then thins into strength while a ratcheting trail protects what's left.

Cost recovery   sell 40% at 1.6×   most of the stake off the table
Ladder          25% at 2.5×  ·  15% at 4×  ·  10% at 8×  ·  5% at 20×
Trailing stop   armed only after the first rung, tightening 45% → 22%
Hard stop       −45% from entry
Time stop       15 min unless already +12%
Dump detector   4σ Shewhart control chart on log returns
Liquidity       immediate exit if the pool halves or becomes unexitable

against a path peaking at 10.2× and returning to 1.0×  →  realises 3.59×

The trail is deliberately not armed before the first rung. New tokens wick 40% routinely, and an early trailing stop converts survivable noise into realised losses.

When to buy

Sniping the mint loses money

The obvious objection to waiting is that you miss the move — the median graduating token graduates in 4.4 minutes. So each entry age was backtested in isolation rather than argued about.

At 30 seconds there is nothing to measure and the bot is buying at the base rate, where the median token never moves. By half an hour the move has already happened and what remains is the distribution. The information arrives in between, and peaks near three minutes.

What narrowing the window bought

Both tails were losing configurations, not merely weaker ones, so the entry window was cut to 60–300 seconds. Headline ROI fell from 94% to 72% — and the 95% confidence interval on expectancy per trade moved from [0.002, 0.694] to [0.052, 0.640], positive across its whole range. Fewer marginal trades, a more reliable edge.

The audit

The spectacular version was wrong

An earlier run of this same pipeline reported +279% with a profit factor of 18, led by a 136× trade. Auditing the individual trades killed it.

That token's price rose roughly 190-fold while its reported liquidity sat flat at about $72,000 for the entire run. In a constant-product pool the two are mechanically linked — pool value is 2·√(k·P), so a 100× price move must show a ~10× liquidity move. A price that moves while depth does not is a broken field, not a trade. The affected tokens were disproportionately ones mimicking real tickers: GOOGL, MSFT, MRNA, BITMINE.

What the guard does now

Every consecutive pair of snapshots is checked against that square-root relationship. Moves under 3× are never judged, because the relationship is noise at that scale; a single unsupported step above 5× is disqualifying on its own. 6% of mints are dropped, and every analysis path runs behind the same entry point so an artefact cannot quietly become a headline again.

The census barely moved — median peak still 1.000×, 7.94% ever 2× — which is the reassuring part. The artefacts were distorting the strategy results, not the base rates. The strategy results moved by an order of magnitude.

What ships

Four conditions. No fitted weights, no model, nothing to overfit:

holders ≥ 20   ·   liquidity ≥ $3,000   ·   buy/sell ≥ 0.55   ·   market cap ≥ $5,000

All four say the same thing in different words — real participants have arrived and are net buying. Market cap is the one that adds independent information, being nearly uncorrelated with liquidity (r = 0.06).

Read this before believing the 2.41

The gate's thresholds came from univariate analysis of an earlier census, so they are not fully out-of-sample themselves. A sweep of neighbouring values stays profitable — a plateau rather than a spike — but the level above is optimistic by some unmeasured amount.

The whole census is hours, not weeks, from a single market regime. No real transaction has been landed, so failed sends and sandwich attacks on entry are unmodelled. This is a modest, real edge on 142 held-out trades, not a money printer.

The model, and why it is off

Two features that were cheating

Once the census reached ~7,900 decision rows the trainer stopped refusing. Fitted with purged, embargoed, mint-grouped walk-forward splits it reaches 32.2% precision in the top decile against a 5.95% base rate — a 6.65× lift, averaging 2.83× peak in that decile.

Two feature groups were removed even though both cost measured performance:

  • Raw price level. It correlates with the label only because the label is a ratio — a token priced at 1e-9 reaches 2× on an absolute move a token priced at 1e-4 could never make. Keeping it teaches the model to buy small numbers.
  • Observation cadence. obs_age and obs_lag were the top two features after that. They are legitimately known at decision time, but they encode this collector's polling schedule rather than the market. Removing them cost lift 5.90 → 5.78 while the top-decile multiple rose 3.00 → 3.12. The edge was never in the sampling artefact.
Why it still does not ship

A 6.65× out-of-fold lift is real, and the model still is not the default. Adding it to the entry rule raises in-sample ROI to 39.2% and delivers 17.0% out of sample on 36 trades — worse than the four-condition gate on a quarter of the sample. Ranking well is not the same as trading well, and the honest read is that there is not yet enough data to fit it into a live edge.

It stays in the codebase behind --rule scorer, because it may well win once the census is large enough. That is a question for more data, not more tuning.

Architecture

How a token becomes a position

The backtester and the live trader call the same feature builder, the same filters, the same scorer, the same exit function and the same fill maths. A divergence between paper and live is a bug, not a design choice.

  1. DiscoverJupiter's recent-token feed, polled every 12s — catches mints within seconds, carrying creator wallet, prior-mint count, holders and buy/sell counts
  2. TrackAge-tiered re-polling, every 20s under five minutes old, backing off to two hours — 100 mints per request
  3. FeatureBuilt at a fixed token age using only observations at or before it. Fixing the age removes the confound that makes the obvious design leak
  4. FilterCheapest checks first: free arithmetic, then one RugCheck call, then RPC. Rejects live authorities, lethal Token-2022 extensions, concentration, factories, thin pools
  5. ScoreTransparent additive rules, every weight tied to a measured effect. A gradient-boosted model blends in only once the data can train one honestly
  6. SizeFifth-Kelly, capped absolutely and again at 2% of pool depth, scaled by the measured market regime
  7. ExecuteSelf-hosted. No retail platform exposes a supported trading API, and self-hosting saves their ~1% take
  8. ManageLadder, ratcheting trail, hard/time/liquidity stops and a 4σ dump detector, re-evaluated every five seconds

Running it

degen collect                 # build the dataset — run it for weeks
degen baserates               # the honest outcome distribution
degen scan --top 15           # current candidates, with reasons
degen backtest                # evaluate, and read the robustness block
degen train                   # refuses if the data cannot support a model
degen trade --mode paper      # simulated fills against live prices
The sequence that matters

Collect for weeks on an always-on host, then paper trade for a month, then --mode dry to compare real quotes against simulated fills, and only then live with a bankroll far below what you eventually intend. The single biggest weakness of everything above is that the collector has run for hours, not weeks.

Every figure on this page was measured by the system on live Solana data, or measured on-chain directly. Where published research disagreed with our own measurements — a reported 8.94× graduation lift from a Telegram link shows 0.50× in this census — the disagreement is recorded rather than resolved by preference. Expect to lose money: the measured base rate is that the median new token never goes up.
