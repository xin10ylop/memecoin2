# X/Twitter and Telegram alpha as a tradable signal for Solana memecoin trading — 2026 ingestion economics and whether the signal is real

## Executive summary

INGESTION (a): X's official API moved to pure pay-per-usage in 2026 — $0.005 per Post read, $0.010 per User read, hard-capped at 3,000,000 Post reads/month before Enterprise (docs.x.com, verbatim). Filtered Stream IS available on pay-per-use (1 connection, 1,000 rules, 1,024-char rules, 250 posts/sec, ~6-7s P99 latency); the sub-second product (Powerstream) is Enterprise-only. Every delivered stream Post is billed at $0.005, so a broad CA-matching rule is financially ruinous. Resources are deduplicated within a 24h UTC window, which is the single biggest cost lever. Third parties are 30-250x cheaper: Sorsa (ex-TweetScout domain) $49/mo for 10K requests at 20 req/s; twitterapi.io $0.15/1K tweets, QPS tiered by credit balance (3/6/10/20); SocialData $0.0002/item, 120 req/min, plus a webhook Monitoring API at ~$5/monitor/month with explicitly non-real-time ~30s delivery; Apify actors $0.15-$0.40/1K tweets. Nitter's public-instance model is dead (real accounts/session tokens now required); twscrape works but needs your own X account pool. Kaito's formerly-free Yaps endpoint now returns Unauthorized and its Open Protocol doc page 404s. Elfa AI is the only vendor I verified that ships trending *contract addresses* by mention count for both Twitter and Telegram (free tier 1,000 credits/mo, 60 RPM; Grow $290/mo, 40K credits, 120 RPM; PAYG/x402 $0.009/credit, x402 at 1,000 RPM). LunarCrush Individual is $90/mo but only 10 req/min and 2,000 req/day — arithmetically incapable of per-launch polling. Santiment gates real-time API behind Sanbase Max $249/mo (Pro's API data carries a 30-day lag). Moby's moby.trade domain has expired; Alphanomics is showing a maintenance page; ChainEdge publishes no pricing page. For Telegram, Telethon over MTProto is free, sub-second, and the only retail-cost social feed fast enough to matter — but one account can join at most 500 channels/supergroups (1,000 with Premium), which is the real capacity constraint.

DOES IT WORK (b): The measured literature is uniformly hostile to buying at the call. Dhawan & Putniņš (355 pumps) find participants join "despite negative expected returns" with 65% average price distortion. Xu & Livshits (412 Telegram pumps) estimate admins netted 199.52 BTC (~$1.1M) across 348 events at an aggregate 18% insider return, and document a channel that announced the coin *when the price was already at its peak*, making follower profit arithmetically impossible; they also find views-vs-pump-gain correlation of −0.162, i.e. bigger channel does not mean bigger pump. La Morgia et al. show ranked/VIP members receive the signal 1-10 seconds ahead of ordinary members and that abnormal *sell* volume appears at second 19 of the pump — the dump begins while followers are still buying. Clough & Edwards (10,687 events, 765 coins) measure peak at roughly two minutes, average 5-minute max gain of 15.02% on Binance-class venues vs 790.54% on illiquid ones, and −30% relative price one year later. On Solana specifically: the strongest published social result is that advertising a Telegram channel in pump.fun launch metadata raises the fast-regime graduation rate from 0.166% to 1.485% (8.94x, Cox HR 5.40, n=832,941) while advertising a Twitter account barely moves it (0.1486% → 0.2268%, HR 1.30). Both of the best-performing published Solana rug/risk models (MELT, "Catching the Rug", 6.4M tokens) use zero social features — on-chain behavior alone. No published work I could find establishes predictive value for "first mention velocity" on memecoins; the closest honest evidence (Alizadeh et al.) finds a ≥6-hour lag on majors only, and the YouTube finfluencer study finds aggregate video counts Granger-cause price but *top* finfluencers' videos do not. Coordinated bot amplification is real and measurable: 61% of accounts retweeting NFT promotion tweets were bot-like at Botometer t=0.43, and 36% of promoted projects were fraudulent — but Botometer itself is no longer usable, so you must build your own classifier. Verdict: X mention data is a *disqualifier* feature (fake-account detection, bot-amplification detection), not an entry trigger. Telegram presence in launch metadata is a legitimate weak prior. Paid "KOL alpha" products sold to retail are, as far as published measurement goes, unverified marketing.

## Numeric parameters

- **has_telegram in launch metadata — score bonus** = `Apply a positive prior equivalent to ~8.9x lift; only ~2.4% of launches carry it` — Kaplan-Meier on 832,941 pump.fun launches: 1.4850% [1.327, 1.661] graduation with an advertised Telegram channel (n=20,270) vs 0.1661% without (n=812,671), log-rank p<1e-300, multivariate Cox HR 5.402 [4.733, 6.166]. Strongest published social variable by a wide margin. Read as a fast-regime (~6 min) lower bound.
- **has_twitter in launch metadata — score bonus** = `Effectively zero; do NOT use as a positive gate` — 0.2268% [0.214, 0.240] with vs 0.1486% [0.136, 0.163] without; Cox HR only 1.305 [1.192, 1.428]. 528,126 of 832,941 launches (63.4%) advertise one, so it discriminates almost nothing. social_count is also non-monotonic (2 channels = 0.1655%, worse than 1 channel = 0.2293%), confirming the signal is the Telegram indicator alone.
- **All three socials (twitter + website + telegram)** = `1.9186% graduation, n=13,708 (1.65% of launches), 17.4x lift over zero-social 0.1104%` — Same dataset. A very tight, high-prior subset. Useful as a high-conviction bucket given a base rate where only 7.5% of launches ever 2x.
- **Maximum acceptable social-feed latency for an entry trigger** = `None available below ~6 seconds except direct Telegram MTProto (sub-second)` — X Filtered Stream P99 6-7s (X's own docs) = 16-19 Solana slots at the 359-375 ms/slot I measured live via getRecentPerformanceSamples. SocialData monitors ~30s. TGStat index 7s. Telegram Telethon events.NewMessage is push, sub-second. If a signal must trigger an order, it must come from Telethon.
- **Holding-period ceiling if you ever trade a call** = `~120 seconds to peak; ~210 seconds to below the pre-call price` — Clough & Edwards: price 'peaks for around two minutes'. Xu & Livshits case study: 'Three and half minutes after the start of the pump-and-dump, the coin price had dropped below its open price.' Their whole ΔP metric uses a 5-minute post-announcement window.
- **Expected max gain if buying at the call** = `+15.02% average on liquid venues; +790.54% average on illiquid venues; expected value NEGATIVE net of the dump` — Clough & Edwards, 10,687 events across 765 coins, 5-minute max-price window. Dhawan & Putniņš (355 cases) state participants join 'despite negative expected returns' with 65% average price distortion. The 790% figure is on venues where you cannot exit size — exactly the regime your 2%-of-pool-depth cap exists to avoid.
- **Assumed head start held by everyone ahead of you** = `1-10 seconds on tiered Telegram channels; sell-side flow already present at second 19` — La Morgia et al.: per-level advantage 0.5-1 second, maximum advantage 'in the interval between 1 and 10 seconds'; abnormal sell volume of ~10 BTC observed at the 19th second of the OAX pump while members were still buying.
- **Per-channel front-run test threshold** = `Reject a channel if the median (first on-chain buy timestamp − channel post timestamp) is negative` — Xu & Livshits document channels announcing at the peak and pre-announcement price elevation exceeding the pump-window return on Cryptopia. A negative median gap means buys systematically precede the post, i.e. the channel is a distribution venue, not a signal.
- **Do not weight calls by audience size** = `Correlation between announcement views and pump gain = −0.162` — Xu & Livshits. Examples: MST 325 views → 12.6% gain; PARTY 18 views → 533.3% gain. Weight channels by realised forward return of their posted CAs instead.
- **Coordinated-supply threshold** = `36.5% of token supply held by coordinated (bundled) accounts on average` — MELT dataset, 41k+ Solana launches, 200M+ transactions. This is the population mean, so it is the baseline your holder-concentration filter must beat, not a red line. $HAWK had 96% in one cluster; $LIBRA 82%.
- **Buyer-count vs SOL-inflow split in the participation-evidence window** = `Weight non-cohort SOL inflow, not unique buyer count` — Coordinated sniper cohorts produce a contamination-adjusted +16.1% [+13.0%, +19.4%] first-30-minute BUYER-COUNT lift but only +6.3% [−0.5%, +15.1%] SOL-inflow lift, not distinguishable from zero. 7.0% of cohort-treated launches (382/5,419) had zero non-cohort buyers at 30 minutes.
- **Hacked/repurposed X account veto** = `account created_at > 365 days ago AND first crypto-related post < 14 days old AND a long inactivity gap immediately before the crypto pivot` — created_at survives handle repurposing (Sorsa User model); user IDs never change (GET /username-to-id). Bubblemaps' Matt Furie case documents extended prior inactivity plus location mismatch plus four token promotions in one week. Precision/recall of this exact rule is UNMEASURED — treat as a heuristic veto, not a validated classifier.
- **Repeat-promoter veto (strongest documented signature, on-chain, free)** = `Reject if the same wallet cluster appears in the top-10 realised-PnL set for 2+ tokens promoted by the same X handle within 30 days` — Bubblemaps: 'the same wallets tended to top the profit-and-loss tables on each promoted token' across $BOYZ, $MEGUSTA, $DORK, $MUFFIN. This is computable entirely from chain data you already index.
- **Bot-amplification proxy (Botometer replacement)** = `Flag when >50% of distinct engaging accounts have created_at within 90 days, or followers/followings < 0.1, or near-duplicate mention text rate > 30%` — Calibration anchor: 61% of 283,409 sampled NFT-promotion retweeters were bot-like at Botometer t=0.43, and 36% of the promoted projects were fraudulent. Botometer's service is no longer usable post-API-repricing, so these are constructible substitutes. Thresholds are MY proposal, not measured — tune against your own labelled set.
- **Scam-promoter profile calibration point** = `Median 3.7k followers, median 198 likes across first 10 timeline posts` — Saha Roy et al., measured on 382 verified-fraudulent projects detected at 95.2% TPR. Useful as a centroid for 'looks engaged but is manufactured'.
- **Telegram ingestion capacity planning** = `500 channels per account (1,000 with Premium); plan N accounts = ceil(target_channels / 500)` — Hard Telegram platform limit on combined channel + supergroup membership. Also 50 channel/group creations per day, 3 accounts per official client.
- **Per-launch X enrichment cost (1 profile + 20 mentions)** = `$0.110 official X; ~$0.0032 twitterapi.io; ~$0.0042 SocialData; 2 requests on Sorsa` — Official: $0.010 User read + 20 × $0.005 Post read. twitterapi.io: $0.18/1K profiles + $0.15/1K tweets. SocialData: 21 items × $0.0002. At 200 qualifying launches/day: $660/mo vs $19/mo vs $25/mo vs $49/mo flat (Sorsa Starter, 10K requests covers 12K/mo only at Pro $199).
- **Minimum sample before evaluating whether a social feature helped** = `Well above 300 trades; 190 trades was demonstrably too few` — A live 190-trade Solana deployment showed population excess kurtosis 6.61 and skewness −1.21, and removing the top 3 trades (1.6% of sample) flipped cumulative return from +117.7% to unprofitable. Any A/B on a social feature at n<300 is noise.
- **Rejection-tracking benchmark** = `Track forward returns of rejected tokens; 56.25% of rejects (27/48) hit −50% within 6 hours in one measured cohort` — arXiv:2606.08232, 4,874 forward observations across 184 rejection events. The full-cohort figure of 17.9% is a censored lower bound. This is how you prove a filter is net-positive rather than assuming it.

## Traps and failure modes

- Filtered Stream looks free because there is no connection charge — but every Post the stream delivers is billed at $0.005. A broad rule matching pump.fun mentions or base58 CA patterns will deliver tens of thousands of posts a day. At 50,000 posts/day that is $250/day / $7,500/month for data you can get from twitterapi.io for under $20. Never write a high-recall stream rule on the official API.
- The 24-hour UTC deduplication window is the single biggest cost lever on the official API and almost nobody models it. Re-reading the same Post or user within the same UTC day is free; issuing a new search that returns fresh resources is not. Structure monitoring as re-reads, not re-searches.
- Six to seven seconds of P99 stream latency is 16-19 Solana slots (I measured 359-375 ms/slot live). Any strategy whose entry depends on being first to a tweet is already lost on the pay-per-use tier. Powerstream — the only sub-second X product — is Enterprise-only with no published price.
- SocialData's monitoring API says in its own docs 'We do not promise real-time delivery' and recommends against sub-second use. People build trade triggers on it anyway and then attribute the resulting slippage to execution.
- LunarCrush Individual at $90/mo gives 2,000 requests per DAY. That is 1.4/minute. It cannot service a per-launch pipeline, and its universe is curated coins/topics — a 90-second-old Solana mint is not in coverage at all. The marketing does not mention this; the rate-limit table does.
- Santiment Sanbase Pro ($49/mo) explicitly serves API data with a 30-DAY LAG. Only Sanbase Max at $249/mo gives real-time API. Worse: Santiment documents that historical social metric values are recalculated for the previous month when labels or sources change — so a backtest you ran in March is not reproducible in May.
- Kaito's Yaps endpoint is a zombie reference in dozens of tutorials and repos. It now returns {"message":"Unauthorized"} and the Open Protocol doc page 404s. Yaps post-to-earn was sunset in January 2026. No published evidence has ever linked yaps or mindshare to memecoin returns — it is sold as alpha and is unverified marketing.
- Botometer, the tool every bot-detection paper uses (including the 61%-bots NFT result), is no longer operable after X's API repricing. Any 2026 plan that says 'we'll score accounts with Botometer' is dead on arrival; you must build and calibrate your own classifier.
- Nitter's public-instance model is dead — guest tokens expired February 2024 and, per 2025 reporting, guest tokens became fingerprint-bound with datacenter IPs permanently banned. The project is NOT archived, which misleads people into thinking free scraping still works; the README now requires real accounts and session tokens.
- twscrape and self-hosted Nitter shift the cost from dollars to account attrition, residential proxies, and unscheduled 3am breakage when X changes GraphQL. For a production trading loop the $20-40/month saved over twitterapi.io is not worth the outage risk on a component you cannot patch quickly.
- Weighting a call by channel size or view count is backwards: measured correlation between announcement views and pump gain is −0.162, with a 18-view announcement producing 533% and a 325-view announcement producing 12.6%. Every retail 'top KOL' leaderboard product is built on the wrong variable.
- Tiered Telegram channels deliberately give VIPs a 1-10 second head start, and admin/bot sell volume is measurable at second 19 of the pump. You are structurally in the last cohort of any public channel. If you cannot measure the gap between first on-chain buy and the post timestamp, you do not know which cohort you are in.
- Buyer COUNT in the early window is a contaminated metric: coordinated sniper cohorts produce a +16.1% buyer-count lift with a SOL-inflow lift statistically indistinguishable from zero, and 7.0% of cohort-treated launches had zero non-cohort buyers at 30 minutes. If your participation-evidence check counts wallets, you are being farmed by exactly the actors you are trying to avoid.
- The 'hacked account' explanation is itself a red flag pattern, not an exoneration. Bubblemaps documented four promotions in one week, three claimed compromises in one month with no security remediation, and the same wallets topping the PnL table on every promoted token. Treat repeated compromise claims as evidence of insider promotion, not of victimhood.
- Every large-sample study that computes it finds buying at the call is negative-EV: Dhawan & Putniņš state participation occurs 'despite negative expected returns'; Xu & Livshits find insiders taking 18% aggregate while participants absorb the mirror loss, and document a channel that announced at the peak. Any product selling call-following as a strategy is selling exit liquidity.
- The two best published Solana memecoin risk models (MELT, 41k launches / 200M transactions; Catching the Rug, 6.4M tokens) contain literally zero social features. If X and Telegram data were load-bearing, the state of the art would use it. Do not let a social score override an on-chain reject.
- No published work establishes predictive value for 'first mention velocity' on memecoins. The nearest honest results are a ≥6-hour Granger lag on majors only (mixed across coins and periods) and a finding that AGGREGATE finfluencer video counts Granger-cause price while TOP finfluencers' videos do not. Anyone quoting a mention-velocity threshold at you invented it.
- Public channel-size rankings are useless for discovering call channels: TGStat's crypto leaderboard is dominated by tap-to-earn and airdrop farms (Blum 15.3M, Major 11.8M, DOGS 7.4M subscribers). The real Solana call channels are private or handle-rotating. Bootstrap your channel list from t.me links in launch metadata instead.
- Moby (moby.trade domain expired), Alphanomics (maintenance page), and ChainEdge (no pricing page, no public API docs) are not integrable in August 2026. Do not architect around any of them.
- The pay-per-use Post-read cap is 3,000,000/month per the official docs, not the 2,000,000 that several 2026 blog posts repeat, and the ubiquitous '$42,000/month Enterprise' figure appears in no primary source I could find. Legacy Basic $200 / Pro $5,000 tiers are claimed by third parties to survive for grandfathered accounts but appear nowhere on docs.x.com.
- Elfa's keyword-mentions endpoint returns NO raw tweet text — only a link field — and caps metadata.total at 10,000. If your model needs text (duplicate-content detection, LLM sentiment), you must pay a second vendor to fetch the tweets, doubling the per-token cost.
- The pump.fun graduation-rate study whose Telegram effect you would rely on is explicitly scoped as a ~6-minute fast-regime lower bound, because its collector polled a top-50-newest endpoint and lost mints that scrolled off. The author documents this and withdraws an attempted correction that failed on-chain verification (0 of 100 sampled mints). Use the direction and the relative ordering of the hazard ratios; do not use the absolute rates as 24-hour probabilities.
- At n<300 trades on a distribution with excess kurtosis 6.6, any evaluation of whether a social feature helped is statistical theater. One documented deployment flipped from +117.7% cumulative to unprofitable when the top 3 of 190 trades were removed. Pre-commit sample size and pre-register the test before you add an X feature to the scorer.

## Findings

### Detecting a repurposed/hacked X account: the reliable primitives are created_at, user_id stability, and profile-change events — not follower count

Mechanics verified from Sorsa's API docs: the User object exposes created_at (ISO 8601 account creation date) which does NOT change when a handle is repurposed, while GET /username-to-id notes 'Unlike usernames, user IDs never change.' The Follower object carries a follow date, so you can measure the age distribution of the follower cohort. SocialData's Monitoring API has a dedicated 'User Profile' monitor type that fires a webhook 'when profile information like bio or name' changes, ~30s latency, ~$4.99/month for up to 10 monitors. Bubblemaps' 'Hacked or Habits' case study on the Matt Furie account documents the real-world pattern: four tokens ($BOYZ, $MEGUSTA, $DORK, $MUFFIN) promoted within a single week in 2026, posts deleted with compromise claims, 'the same wallets tended to top the profit-and-loss tables on each promoted token', three compromises in one month with no security upgrade, location mismatches and extended prior inactivity, account went private afterward. **Actionable:** Concrete veto rule: reject when the token's advertised X handle satisfies (created_at older than 365 days) AND (the account's earliest crypto-related post is younger than 14 days) AND (a long posting gap exists in the timeline immediately before the crypto pivot). ### A 15-day live Solana deployment shows filter-stack results are structurally fragile at small sample sizes

Kamat, arXiv:2606.08232, 190 paper trades March 29 - April 12 2026: 40.5% win rate, mean per-trade +0.62%, cumulative +117.7% (net SOL +0.039), skewness -1.21, excess kurtosis 6.61. **Actionable:** Directly transferable methodology: track the forward returns of tokens your filter REJECTS, not just the ones you buy. 56.25% of rejects hitting -50% within six hours is how you prove a filter is net-positive rather than assuming it.
