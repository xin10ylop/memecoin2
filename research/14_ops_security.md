# OpSec, wallet architecture, regulatory/tax posture, and catastrophic failure modes for an individually-operated automated memecoin trading bot (Solana primary; Base/BSC secondary)

## Executive summary

Key correction to conventional wisdom: AWS KMS now natively supports Ed25519 (key spec ECC_NIST_EDWARDS25519, signing algo ED25519_SHA_512 with MessageType:RAW), so you can hold a Solana hot-wallet key in an HSM-backed KMS and never have raw key material in your process — this was impossible until recently and most ecosystem code (including the reference Chainstack pump.fun bot) still does `private_key: "${SOLANA_PRIVATE_KEY}"` from a .env file. GCP Cloud KMS supports the same via EC_SIGN_ED25519 at SOFTWARE or HSM protection level. Cost is trivial ($1/key/month + $0.15/10k Sign calls) and the default shared ECC quota is 1,000 rps, far above what a memecoin bot needs; the real cost is added signing latency in a latency-critical path, which argues for KMS on the treasury/sweeper key and a short-lived encrypted-keystore key in-process for the sniper. Private key compromise is the single largest loss category in crypto: Chainalysis attributes 43.8% of the $2.2B stolen in 2024 to private key compromise. On wallet-per-trade: it does not work as anti-tracking. Any wallet's complete history is enumerable by anyone at 1,000 signatures per `getSignaturesForAddress` call with no auth on free public RPC (verified live), and a fresh wallet funded from your treasury is clustered to you in one hop by any indexer, so you pay real cost for near-zero privacy — 0.00203928 SOL ATA rent per token (verified on-chain via getMinimumBalanceForRentExemption(165) = 2,039,280 lamports), plus two extra transactions, plus, critically, IRS §1.1012-1(j) applies specific-identification and FIFO on a *per-wallet* basis for all dispositions on or after Jan 1 2025, so N wallets means N basis pools to track. Use 2-4 rotating hot wallets with a hard per-wallet balance cap and a cold vault instead. On MEV: sandwiching is industrialized and specifically targets your asset class — one program ("Vpe") executed 1.55M sandwich transactions in 30 days at 88.9% success for $13.43M, averaging $8.67 profit per sandwich, and 16 of the top 20 sandwiched tokens were Pump.fun launches. The mechanism that makes you the victim is your own slippage setting: Helius documents that sandwiched users are "almost always" filled at exactly their max slippage, and the reference bot ships a 30% default. On honeypots: on Solana the trap is not a classic EVM sell-tax but Token-2022 extensions — TransferHook (creator program can reject any transfer), PermanentDelegate (creator can burn/transfer from any account), DefaultAccountState=frozen, and a TransferFee whose rate the fee authority can raise after you buy. Reject any mint carrying these. Legally, for an individual trading their own money the posture is clear and favorable: FinCEN ruling FIN-2014-R002 holds that a person trading virtual currency solely for their own account is not a money transmitter, so no MSB registration (18 USC 1960 carries 5 years and (b)(1)(B) requires no knowledge of the registration duty, so this exemption matters); and SEC staff stated Feb 27 2025 that meme coins as described are not securities. What is illegal is unchanged by automation: CFTC Rule 180.1/180.2 reach fraud and price manipulation "in connection with any... contract of sale of any commodity in interstate commerce," i.e. spot crypto, which covers wash trading to fake volume, insider trading your own launches, and pump-and-dump promotion. Tax is the underrated operational risk: 26 USC 1091(a) applies only to "shares of stock or securities," so crypto wash sales are currently allowed (verified verbatim), but per-wallet basis tracking plus 10k+ trades/year makes tooling mandatory and specific identification must be made "no later than the date and time of sale," which means your bot must emit the tax lot record at execution time — you cannot reconstruct it later. Cost discipline matters more than alpha at small size: a round trip with a 95th-percentile Jito tip costs roughly 0.00064 SOL net of ATA rent reclamation, but if you never close ATAs you strand 0.00203928 SOL per token touched (~20.4 SOL across 10,000 tokens).

## Numeric parameters

- **top_10_non_lp_holder_pct_max** = `25%` — Reject if sum of top-10 holders' pct, after excluding addresses present in RugCheck knownAccounts (type AMM) and in lockerOwners, exceeds 25%. RugCheck returns topHolders[].pct and the knownAccounts label map directly, so this is computable in one API call.
- **single_non_lp_holder_pct_max** = `10%` — A single non-LP wallet above 10% can exit into your position alone. Complements the aggregate top-10 rule, which a well-distributed insider set can pass.
- **freeze_authority / mint_authority** = `must be null (or 11111111111111111111111111111111)` — Non-null freezeAuthority lets the creator freeze your token account so you cannot sell; non-null mintAuthority lets them dilute you to zero. Both exposed directly on the RugCheck report and readable from the mint account.
- **transfer_fee_authority** = `must be null even when transferFee.pct == 0` — Token-2022 TransferFee rate is mutable by the fee authority after launch. A 0% fee at buy time is meaningless if the authority survives. Verified: report returns transferFee {pct, maxAmount, authority}.
- **banned_token_2022_extensions** = `TransferHook, PermanentDelegate, NonTransferable, DefaultAccountState` — TransferHook = arbitrary programmable sell-blocking; PermanentDelegate = creator can burn/transfer from any account (confiscation); NonTransferable = soul-bound; DefaultAccountState = new accounts frozen by default. No legitimate memecoin needs any of these.
- **max_slippage_bps** = `1500 bps (15%) hard cap; target = computed price impact + 300-500 bps` — Sandwichers fill you at exactly your max slippage (Helius). The widely-copied reference config's 3000 bps (30%) default hands 30% of every trade to an attacker while the tx still succeeds. Derive from pool depth, never a constant.
- **expected_sandwich_loss_per_public_swap** = `~0.0425 SOL (~$8.67)` — Measured average profit per sandwich by the dominant Solana sandwich program (Helius MEV report). Subtract this from expected value on any public-path swap when sizing; it implies a hard floor on viable position size.
- **jito_tip_selection** = `p75 (1.2348e-5 SOL) routine, p95 (2.129e-4 SOL) competitive; min 1,000 lamports` — Live values from https://bundles.jito.wtf/api/v1/bundles/tip_floor at 2026-08-23T17:13Z. Poll every 5-15s and set dynamically; hardcoding a tip either overpays 40x or never lands.
- **jito_rate_limit** = `1 req/sec per IP per region` — Hard documented limit, 429 on exceed. Fan out across the 8 regional endpoints (separate buckets) if you need more throughput. Max 5 transactions per bundle.
- **ata_rent_per_token** = `2,039,280 lamports (0.00203928 SOL); Token-2022 2,074,080 lamports` — Verified on-chain via getMinimumBalanceForRentExemption(165)/(170). Unreclaimed across 10,000 tokens = 20.39 SOL stranded. Make closeAccount mandatory (cleanup: after_sell) plus a nightly sweeper.
- **solana_base_fee** = `5,000 lamports per signature (0.000005 SOL)` — Fixed per-signature cost; multiply by signature count. Paid even on failed/reverted transactions, which is why revert-heavy configs bleed.
- **compute_unit_limits** = `buy 100,000 CU, sell 60,000 CU; max 1.4M CU/tx, 48M CU/block, 200,000 CU default if unset` — Reference-bot operational defaults for pump.fun. Setting an explicit, tight CU limit both lowers cost and raises effective priority; SetComputeUnitLimit must be placed before other instructions or the tx fails.
- **priority_fee_hard_cap** = `1,000,000 micro-lamports/CU (= 0.0001 SOL on a 100k CU buy)` — Reference-bot hard_cap. Note this equals the reference buy_amount of 0.0001 SOL — a 100% fee-to-notional ratio. Always enforce a cap so a fee spike cannot drain the wallet in fees alone.
- **min_position_size** = `≥ 2 SOL for public-path swaps (or use private routing below that)` — Derived: round-trip network cost ~0.000636 SOL at p95 tip plus ~0.0425 SOL expected sandwich loss plus protocol fees. Below ~2 SOL the expected sandwich loss alone exceeds 2% of notional.
- **client_rate_limit_rps** = `25 rps token bucket with exponential backoff on 429` — The reference bot's node.max_rps default. Must be set below your provider tier (Helius Free 10, Developer 50, Business 200; Chainstack Solana Developer 5, Growth 50). Retrying 429s tightly is how you get banned rather than throttled.
- **sendTransaction_rps_by_tier** = `Helius: 1 (Free) / 5 ($49) / 50 ($499) / 100 ($999)` — The actual binding constraint on a sniper, not general RPC rps. Budget the $499 Business tier (~$5,988/yr) as the realistic floor and treat it as a fixed cost the strategy must clear.
- **curve_refresh_budget** = `2.0 seconds, skip token on timeout` — Reference-bot pattern: re-read the bonding curve at `processed` before building the buy so fee_recipient/creator_vault/quote_mint are current; if unreadable in budget, SKIP rather than submit with guessed defaults ('tends to revert on-chain'). Fail closed.
- **getSignaturesForAddress_limit** = `1000 max per call` — Verified: limit=1001 returns error -32602. Governs both your own indexing cost and how cheaply an adversary enumerates your wallet — the latter is why per-trade wallets don't buy privacy.
- **hot_wallet_balance_cap** = `≤ 2-5% of total capital per hot wallet, swept above threshold` — Blast-radius containment is the only control you have without a policy engine (plain KMS cannot restrict what a transaction does, only who may sign). Given 43.8% of 2024's $2.2B in thefts came from private key compromise, size the hot wallet at what you can afford to lose entirely.
- **hot_wallet_count** = `2-4 rotating, NOT one per trade` — Balances blast radius against (a) per-wallet ATA rent, (b) per-wallet IRS basis pools under §1.1012-1(j), and (c) matching Chainalysis wash-trading Heuristic 2, which flags a controller managing 5+ addresses with <5% difference between aggregate buys and sells.
- **daily_loss_circuit_breaker** = `halt at -15% of allocated capital in 24h, or 10 consecutive losing trades` — Directly addresses the 'buys the same rug 200 times' mode. A stateless bot has no memory of losing; the breaker supplies it. Must require manual re-arm, not auto-resume.
- **per_creator_and_per_mint_spend_cap** = `1 buy per mint ever; N SOL lifetime cap per creator address` — Serial ruggers relaunch the same asset under a new mint, so a mint-only blacklist is useless. RugCheck returns `creator` and `creatorTokens`. Persist blacklists to disk — an in-memory set re-enables every rug on restart.
- **pump_and_dump_base_rate** = `3.59% of launched tokens (74,037 of 2,063,519); median abandonment 0 days` — Chainalysis 2024, EVM chains, using: address removed ≥65% of pool value ($1,000+), pool now inactive, >100 prior transactions. This is a conservative FLOOR — it excludes launches that never reached 100 transactions. Use as a prior for expected win rate, not as the true rug rate.
- **wash_trade_detection_thresholds_to_avoid** = `buy/sell within 25 blocks with <1% volume difference and 3+ matching trades per address; or a controller with 5+ managed addresses and <5% difference between aggregate buys and sells` — Published Chainalysis heuristics. Use defensively (your own multi-wallet flows must not match Heuristic 2) and offensively (filter out pools whose volume is manufactured).
- **kms_ecc_signing_quota** = `1,000 rps shared per account/region (adjustable); $0.15/10k requests; $1/key/month` — Far above a memecoin bot's needs, so KMS is not a throughput constraint — only a latency one. Cache GetPublicKey at startup rather than calling it per trade.
- **cost_basis_regime_date** = `per-wallet specific-ID/FIFO for all dispositions on or after 2025-01-01` — §1.1012-1(j) of the 2024 final regs (T.D. 10000); Rev. Proc. 2024-28 provides the one-time transition safe harbor as of Jan 1 2025 and cannot be applied to units acquired on or after that date. Specific identification must be made 'no later than the date and time of sale' — emit the tax lot synchronously at execution.

## Traps and failure modes

- CATASTROPHIC #1 — Hot wallet key exfiltration (43.8% of the $2.2B stolen in 2024 was private key compromise). Vectors: key in .env committed to git or baked into a container layer; key in a crash traceback or log line; key in shell history or process argv; a malicious npm/pip dependency; a 'helpful' Discord/GitHub-issue support scam (the reference repo warns its issue tracker is 'regularly targeted by scam bots... after your private keys'). MITIGATION: AWS KMS ECC_NIST_EDWARDS25519 or GCP EC_SIGN_ED25519 so no raw key exists in-process; if impossible, an age/sops-encrypted keystore decrypted to an mlock'd buffer at startup with the passphrase from systemd-creds. Cap the hot wallet at 2-5% of capital, sweep above threshold to a cold vault on a hardware wallet, and pin/audit dependencies. Never paste a key anywhere.
- CATASTROPHIC #2 — Buying the same rug repeatedly (the classic 'bought the same 3 rugs 200 times'). Root cause is statelessness: the bot has no memory of losing, so it re-executes an identical losing trade at machine speed. With a base rate of 3.59% of tokens showing pump-and-dump patterns and a MEDIAN abandonment of 0 days, this compounds fast. MITIGATION: persistent (on-disk, survives restart) blacklist keyed on mint AND creator address AND metadata-image hash — serial ruggers relaunch the same asset with a new mint; one-buy-per-mint-ever; lifetime SOL cap per creator; daily loss circuit breaker at -15% or 10 consecutive losses requiring MANUAL re-arm.
- CATASTROPHIC #3 — Systematic sandwiching hidden by your own high slippage. Because sandwiched transactions SUCCEED, this never appears in error logs; you just underperform by ~0.0425 SOL per swap forever. A 30% slippage default (the widely-copied reference value) authorizes a 30% haircut on every trade. MITIGATION: derive slippage from measured pool depth (impact + 3-5pp, hard cap 15%); route via Jito bundle with bundleOnly=true and the jitodontfront marker; log realized-vs-expected fill on EVERY trade and alert when rolling median realized slippage approaches configured max.
- CATASTROPHIC #4 — Token-2022 honeypot: you buy successfully and cannot sell. On Solana the trap is not an EVM sell tax but TransferHook (creator program rejects your transfer), PermanentDelegate (creator burns/transfers your balance outright), DefaultAccountState=frozen, classic freezeAuthority, or a TransferFee whose authority raises the rate after you enter. MITIGATION: deterministic pre-buy rejection on the mint account for all of these; reject transferFee.authority as non-null even at 0% because the rate is mutable; reject non-null freezeAuthority and mintAuthority. Implement as a pure function over on-chain mint data so it cannot fail open on an API outage.
- CATASTROPHIC #5 — RPC/data-provider cost blowup or ban. Free tiers allow 1 sendTransaction/sec (Helius Free) and 5 rps general RPC (Chainstack Solana Developer); a naive polling loop burns a month of credits in hours, then either bills overage at $5/million credits or gets the key banned. Tight 429 retry loops cause bans rather than throttling. MITIGATION: client-side token bucket (25 rps default) below your tier's limit, exponential backoff with jitter on 429, credits-consumed-per-trade as a monitored metric with a regression alert, a hard monthly spend cap, and a fallback provider. Budget the $499/mo Business tier as the realistic floor and require the strategy to clear ~$6k/yr fixed cost.
- CATASTROPHIC #6 — Silent breakage from an upstream on-chain program upgrade. Documented real example: pump.fun's 2026-04-28 fee-recipient change altered required account lists; separately the Sept 1 move to a market-cap-tiered dynamic fee structure made two new accounts mandatory. A bot with hardcoded fee bps or account lists starts reverting every transaction — burning 5,000 lamports base fee plus priority fee per attempt — while appearing 'up'. MITIGATION: read feeConfig on-chain per trade instead of hardcoding; pin and vendor the IDL at a recorded commit; assert at startup that the program's upgrade slot matches what you tested; circuit-break on N consecutive identical on-chain reverts.
- CATASTROPHIC #7 — Stranded ATA rent bleeding capital invisibly. Every (wallet, mint) pair locks 2,039,280 lamports (0.00203928 SOL, verified on-chain). Never closing them costs 20.39 SOL across 10,000 tokens — and a per-trade-wallet architecture multiplies this. Dust from a partial fill blocks closeAccount, so 'I enabled cleanup' is not sufficient. MITIGATION: cleanup mode after_sell PLUS a nightly sweeper across all wallets that burns sub-threshold dust then closes; track 'stranded rent' as a metric with an alert above 0.5 SOL.
- CATASTROPHIC #8 — Fee-to-notional inversion at small size. The reference config pairs buy_amount 0.0001 SOL with a 0.0001 SOL priority fee and a 0.00203928 SOL ATA rent — fees are 20x+ the trade. Add ~0.0425 SOL expected sandwich loss and small positions are mathematically unprofitable regardless of signal quality. MITIGATION: enforce a minimum position size derived from total round-trip cost (base + priority + tip + unreclaimed rent + protocol fees + slippage + expected MEV loss); log cost-to-notional per trade and refuse trades above threshold. Do not tune the strategy before fixing the cost structure.
- CATASTROPHIC #9 — Tax record loss on 10k+ trades. §1.1012-1(j) requires per-wallet specific-ID/FIFO for all dispositions on or after Jan 1 2025, and adequate identification must be made 'no later than the date and time of sale' — you CANNOT reconstruct specific identification after the fact. Multi-wallet architectures multiply the basis pools. Compounding issues: SPL spam airdrops create thousands of phantom events; failed transactions burn fees that are costs not disposals; aggregator routes import as multiple hops; ATA open/close moves SOL that misparses as income. MITIGATION: emit an immutable tax-lot record SYNCHRONOUSLY with each execution (wallet, mint, qty, USD price at execution, timestamp, signature, lots identified against) to append-only storage — never a nightly batch that can fail silently. Treat your execution log as the system of record and feed tax software by CSV rather than trusting its Solana indexer. Verify plan transaction limits before committing (I could not verify Koinly/CoinTracker caps — their pages block automated access). Reconcile year one with a crypto-specialist CPA.
- CATASTROPHIC #10 — Crossing a legal bright line, usually by accident. The exposures that actually bite an individual: (a) WASH TRADING — routing buy and sell legs of the same position through two wallets you control against the same pool, prohibited by CFTC Rule 180.1/180.2 which reach 'any commodity in interstate commerce' i.e. spot crypto, and trivially detectable (matched buy/sell within 25 blocks, <1% volume difference); (b) INSIDER TRADING YOUR OWN LAUNCHES — the SEC's Feb 27 2025 memecoin statement expressly does not shield fraud and reserves other agencies' authority; (c) UNDISCLOSED PROMOTION of tokens you hold; (d) UNREGISTERED MSB — 18 USC 1960, 5 years, and subsection (b)(1)(A) explicitly removes the knowledge defense for state licensing, triggered the moment you accept anyone else's funds; (e) FRONT-RUNNING OTHER USERS' ORDERS if you ever operate a relay, Telegram bot, or aggregator that sees third-party flow. MITIGATION: stay strictly own-account (FIN-2014-R002 confirms own-account trading is not money transmission); never accept a friend's SOL; disable any third-party bot's 'volume boosting' feature; if you launch a token, your bot must not trade it; keep wallet count low so your funding graph does not resemble Chainalysis wash-trade Heuristic 2 (a controller with 5+ managed addresses whose buys and sells net out).
- TRAP — Per-trade wallets are a false solution. They cost ATA rent + two extra transactions + N basis pools under §1.1012-1(j), and deliver almost no privacy: any wallet's full history is enumerable unauthenticated at 1000 signatures/call, and each child wallet is linked to your treasury in one funding hop by the exact clustering RugCheck already ships (insiderNetworks type:'transfer', graphInsidersDetected). Use 2-4 rotating wallets for blast-radius containment; randomize trade sizes so your positions aren't fingerprintable by a constant buy_amount.
- TRAP — Fail-open filters. If RugCheck 429s (15 req/sec unauthenticated) or GoPlus times out and your code treats 'no risk data' as 'no risk', your safety layer silently disappears at exactly the moment of highest load (a launch burst). Every external safety check must fail CLOSED — skip the trade. Prefer on-chain-derived checks (mint authorities, Token-2022 extensions) over API-derived ones for anything load-bearing, since they cannot be rate limited.
- TRAP — Architecting discovery around getProgramAccounts. It works in development against a small program and is rejected or times out in production against pump.fun's 10M+ accounts, 'no matter which filters you pass' — no provider serves it. Use Geyser/Yellowstone gRPC streaming or filtered subscriptions.
- TRAP — Using the ED25519_PH_SHA_512 AWS KMS algorithm for Solana. It requires MessageType:DIGEST and AWS still applies its own SHA-512 prehash, so the input is hashed twice and the signature will not verify against the Solana transaction. Use ED25519_SHA_512 with MessageType:RAW. AWS explicitly warns the two 'cannot be used interchangeably.'
- TRAP — Assuming KMS contains a compromise. KMS answers only 'may this principal sign?', not 'is this transaction safe?' An attacker with your bot's IAM credentials can sign a transfer of the entire hot wallet. Either use a policy engine that parses the transaction (Turnkey runs a Solana parser in-enclave with IDL-aware policies) or rely on a hard hot-wallet balance cap. Do not rely on KMS alone as the containment control.
- TRAP — Trusting reported volume as a signal. Chainalysis found up to $2.57B of suspected wash trading on Ethereum/BSC/Base in 2024, concentrated in only 0.2-0.3% of pools. A volume-triggered entry can be measuring a bot talking to itself. Compute a funder-deduplicated real-volume estimate before acting on any volume signal.
- TRAP — Assuming crypto wash sales stay legal. 26 USC 1091(a) covers only 'shares of stock or securities' (verified verbatim), so harvesting works today — but this is a well-known legislative gap repeatedly proposed for closure. Do not build a strategy whose profitability depends on it, and re-check each tax year. If a token you trade were deemed a security, 1091 would apply to it.
- TRAP — Self-electing 475(f) mark-to-market. Genuinely unresolved whether a spot-crypto-only trader qualifies as a 'trader in commodities' under 475(e)(2), the election is generally due by the prior year's return due date, and it is hard to revoke. Marked low confidence deliberately — get a specialist opinion in Q4, do not infer it from this research.
- TRAP — Relying on a MiCA scope argument if EU-resident. Whether Title VI reaches a natural person trading own-account on a permissionless AMM (rather than a CASP-operated platform) is not resolved by the text I reviewed. It does not matter operationally: the prohibited conduct is illegal under CFTC rules regardless, so compliant behavior is identical. Get local counsel if the question matters to you.

## Findings

### AWS KMS natively supports Ed25519 signing, making HSM-backed Solana signing possible without raw key material in your process

Key spec ECC_NIST_EDWARDS25519 (ed25519), signing and verification only. Two algorithms: ED25519_SHA_512 (NIST FIPS 186-5 §7.6) which REQUIRES MessageType:RAW, and ED25519_PH_SHA_512 (§7.8, HashEdDSA) which requires MessageType:DIGEST. AWS docs explicitly warn these 'cannot be used interchangeably.' Solana signs the raw serialized message, so you must use ED25519_SHA_512 + MessageType:RAW. Note the PH variant double-hashes (once by you, once by KMS). GetPublicKey returns the DER-wrapped Ed25519 public key; strip the 12-byte SPKI prefix to get the 32-byte Solana pubkey. Quotas: 'Cryptographic operations (ECC and SM2) request rate' = 1,000 rps shared per account/region, adjustable via Service Quotas. Cost: $1/month per key + $0.15 per 10,000 asymmetric requests (vs $0.03/10k for symmetric); free tier 20,000 requests/month.

**Actionable:** Create one KMS key per wallet with KeySpec=ECC_NIST_EDWARDS25519, KeyUsage=SIGN_VERIFY. Sign via kms.Sign(KeyId, Message=tx.message.serialize(), MessageType='RAW', SigningAlgorithm='ED25519_SHA_512'), then attach the 64-byte signature with tx.addSignature(pubkey, sig). Cache GetPublicKey at startup (don't call it per trade). Budget one extra network round trip (~10-40ms same-region) per signature — acceptable for the treasury/sweeper and for exits, marginal for first-slot snipes. Gate withdrawals with a KMS key policy + grants so a compromised bot host can sign trades but a separate principal is required to change policy.

`confidence=high` · source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/kms/latest/developerguide/asymmetric-key-specs.html

### An individual trading their own money with a bot is NOT a money transmitter and does not need MSB registration — this is settled in a FinCEN ruling

Generic wire fraud (18 USC 1343) applies independently to a scheme to obtain money by material misrepresentation or half-truth. In the EU, MiCA Title VI Article 89 prohibits dealing on inside information — 'information of a precise nature' not publicly available with potential significance for price — and Article 91 prohibits market manipulation including wash trades ('executing orders with no genuine market intent') and conduct creating false or misleading impressions of supply, demand or price.

**Actionable:** Hard rules: (1) if you launch a token, do not have your bot buy it, and do not pre-position in wallets you control; (2) do not trade ahead of information you possess non-publicly about a launch, listing, or announcement; (3) do not promote a token you hold without clear, prominent disclosure of your position and any compensation; (4) do not front-run other users' orders — note that if your bot ever sees another party's order flow (e.g. you operate a relay, a Telegram bot, or an aggregator) and trades ahead of it, that is a different and much worse legal posture than generic public-mempool competition.

`confidence=high` · source: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32023R1114

### MiCA market abuse scope for a DEX-only memecoin trader is genuinely ambiguous

MiCA Title VI applies to crypto-assets 'admitted to trading on trading platforms' (Article 86). The regulation's obligations are framed around 'issuers of crypto-assets and crypto-asset service providers', and the recitals note MiCA deliberately uses 'bespoke rules' rather than applying all of Regulation (EU) No 596/2014 because crypto participants are 'very often SMEs.' The source text reviewed does not explicitly resolve whether a natural person trading their own account on a permissionless AMM (not a CASP-operated platform) is within scope.

**Actionable:** Do not rely on a scope argument. The prohibited conduct (wash trading, insider dealing, manipulation) is illegal in the US under CFTC rules regardless of the MiCA analysis, so the compliant behavior is identical either way. If you are EU-resident and the answer matters to you, get local counsel — I could not verify the own-account/DEX scope question from primary text.

`confidence=low` · source: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32023R1114

### The wash sale rule does NOT currently apply to crypto — verified from the statute text, not commentary

26 USC 1091(a) verbatim: 'In the case of any loss claimed to have been sustained from any sale or other disposition of SHARES OF STOCK OR SECURITIES where it appears that, within a period beginning 30 days before the date of such sale or disposition and ending 30 days after such date, the taxpayer has acquired... substantially identical stock or securities, then no deduction shall be allowed under section 165 unless the taxpayer is a dealer in stock or securities...' The statute covers stock or securities and contracts/options to acquire them. It does not mention digital assets or commodities. The IRS treats digital assets as PROPERTY, not currency and not securities.

**Actionable:** Crypto tax-loss harvesting is currently legal: you can sell at a loss and rebuy immediately without the loss being disallowed. Two caveats. (1) This is a legislative gap that has been repeatedly proposed for closure — do not build a strategy that only works if it persists, and re-check before each tax year. (2) If a specific token you trade were deemed a security, 1091 would bite for that token; the SEC memecoin statement helps here but is not dispositive. Also note the economic-substance and step-transaction doctrines still exist for contrived arrangements.

`confidence=high` · source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1091

### As of Jan 1 2025 cost basis must be tracked PER WALLET, and specific identification must be made at or before the moment of sale — this directly penalizes multi-wallet architectures

IRS Rev. Proc. 2024-28 and the 2024 final regulations (T.D. 10000, filed June 28 2024): '§1.1012-1(h) and (j) of the 2024 final regulations will apply to all acquisitions and dispositions of digital assets on or after January 1, 2025' and they 'apply the specific identification or FIFO rules to units held within a single wallet or account.' The revenue procedure exists precisely to transition taxpayers off the 'universal or multi-wallet approach' many had inferred from FAQs 39-41. Rev. Proc. 2024-28 provides a one-time safe harbor to allocate unused basis to wallets as of Jan 1 2025, requires the allocation be 'reasonable' (section 5.02), and states the safe harbor 'may not' be applied to units acquired on or after Jan 1 2025. On timing, adequate identification requires action 'no later than the date and time of sale.' Related: brokers report gross proceeds on Form 1099-DA from Jan 1 2025 and basis on certain transactions from Jan 1 2026.

**Actionable:** This is the strongest practical argument against per-trade wallets, stronger than the on-chain cost. Every wallet is a separate basis pool with its own FIFO queue. Design implication: your bot MUST write an immutable tax-lot record at execution time containing wallet address, mint, quantity, USD price at execution, timestamp, tx signature, and the lot(s) identified against on a sale — you cannot reconstruct 'specific identification' after the fact because the regs require it at or before the sale, and defaulting to FIFO per wallet may be materially worse. Emit this to append-only storage synchronously with the trade, not as a nightly batch that can silently fail.

`confidence=high` · source: https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-24-28.pdf

### Section 475(f) mark-to-market election is available to traders in securities and commodities — its availability to a crypto-only trader is unresolved

26 USC 475(f)(1) covers 'a person who is engaged in a trade or business as a trader in securities'; 475(f)(2) covers 'a person who is engaged in a trade or business as a trader in commodities.' 475(e)(2) defines commodity to include actively traded commodities, notional principal contracts on commodities, and derivatives in commodities. Digital assets are classified by the IRS as property; whether a memecoin is a 'commodity' for 475(e)(2) purposes, and whether a bot operator meets the (judicially developed, not statutory) 'trader in a trade or business' standard, are both unsettled.

**Actionable:** Do not self-elect 475(f) based on this. If it applied it would be significant (ordinary loss treatment, no capital loss $3,000 cap, and wash sales become moot) but the election is generally due by the original due date of the PRIOR year's return, is difficult to revoke, and the crypto eligibility question is genuinely open. This is a 'talk to a crypto-specialist CPA in Q4, not in April' item. Flagged low confidence deliberately — I did not find authority applying 475(f) to spot crypto.

`confidence=low` · source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/475

### Solana per-transaction cost components, measured, so you can compute a true break-even

Verified/derived: base fee 5,000 lamports per signature (0.000005 SOL). ATA rent-exempt minimum 2,039,280 lamports (0.00203928 SOL), reclaimable via closeAccount at zero balance. Priority fee = compute_unit_limit × compute_unit_price(micro-lamports) / 1e6 lamports; max budget 1.4M CU per transaction, 48M CU per block, default 200,000 CU if unset. Reference bot defaults: buy 100,000 CU, sell 60,000 CU, fixed CU price 1,000,000 micro-lamports with hard_cap 1,000,000 — that is 100,000 × 1e6 / 1e6 = 100,000 lamports = 0.0001 SOL priority fee on a buy. Jito tip live percentiles (2026-08-23): p50 5.142e-6, p75 1.2348e-5, p95 2.129e-4, p99 1.0e-3 SOL. Derived round-trip at p95 tip: buy (0.000005 + 0.0001 + 0.0002129 + 0.00203928) + sell (0.000005 + 0.0001 + 0.0002129) − ATA reclaim (0.00203928) ≈ 0.000636 SOL, EXCLUDING pump.fun protocol/creator fees and slippage.

**Actionable:** Note the reference config's own numbers are self-defeating: buy_amount 0.0001 SOL with a 0.0001 SOL priority fee is a 100% fee-to-notional ratio, and the 0.00203928 SOL ATA rent is 20x the trade size. Enforce a minimum position size such that total round-trip cost (base + priority + tip + unreclaimed rent + protocol fees + expected slippage + expected sandwich loss of ~0.04 SOL) is below a target percentage of notional — at ~0.04 SOL expected sandwich loss alone, positions below ~2 SOL are structurally hard to justify on public routes. Compute and log cost-to-notional per trade and alert above threshold.

### Chainalysis wash-trading detection heuristics are published, which tells you exactly what patterns your own bot must avoid emitting

2024, Ethereum + BNB Smart Chain + Base: up to $2.57B suspected wash trading. Heuristic 1 (matched buy/sell): buy and sell within 25 blocks with <1% volume difference, 3+ matching trades per address → $704M, 0.035% of DEX volume (Nov 2024). Heuristic 2 (multi-sender dispersion): controller addresses managing 5+ managed addresses with <5% difference between buy and sell totals → $1.87B, 0.046% of DEX volume. Concentration: only ~1,000-1,800 DEX pools per month showed suspected wash trading, 0.2-0.3% of ~500,000 active pools.

**Actionable:** Two uses. (1) Defensive/legal: your multi-wallet architecture must not accidentally match Heuristic 2 — a treasury funding 5+ trading wallets whose aggregate buys and sells roughly net out is literally the described pattern. This is a real reason to keep the wallet count low (2-4) and to avoid symmetric cross-wallet flows. (2) Offensive/filtering: apply the same heuristics to candidate tokens — a pool whose volume is dominated by addresses with matched buy/sell within 25 blocks has fake volume, and your 'volume' entry signal is measuring a bot talking to itself. Compute a real-volume estimate (unique-funder-deduplicated) before trusting any volume-based signal.
