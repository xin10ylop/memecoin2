# Predictive features and modelling for early-token (Solana memecoin) outcome prediction

## Executive summary

The single most useful artefact found is MELT (arXiv 2602.13480, code at github.com/git-disl/MELT, CC BY-NC 4.0). I cloned it and read src/feat_gen.py line by line, so the 122 feature definitions below are exact code, not paraphrase. It gives you: (a) sniper-supply features at 0s/1s/5s/10s from mint, (b) a union-find entity-resolution over three edge types (same-transaction co-swap, same Jito bundle, same first-funding-signer excluding CEX accounts) that recovers 36.50% of supply as coordinated vs 9-16% from any single channel, (c) first-buyer retention ratios (current_hold / initial_hold for top-1/5/10/20 first buyers) which your feature set lacks entirely, and (d) the concealment-gap deltas (cluster-level topK% minus address-level topK%).

Critical caveat I derived myself from MELT's shipped prediction CSVs (results/*.csv joined to data/label/label.csv): their models are good at avoiding the crash label (P@100 for "not high risk" 0.243 -> 0.82-0.85) but essentially do NOT improve upside. Fraction of top-100-ranked tokens with return_ratio > 1 is 0.08-0.13 vs a base rate of 0.0815, and median return in the top-100 is still -0.21 to -0.33. Their headline "reduces loss from 60.71% to 26.64%" is a loss-reduction claim on a universe that is losing money on average; it is not evidence that a rule-scored long book gets better hit rates. Do not expect a MELT-style risk model to raise your 7.5%-ever-2x number. It will raise your average exit price.

Second-order findings that should change your priors: (1) Marino et al. (2602.14860) find that for a fixed bonding-curve SOL level, tokens reaching it in FEWER trades graduate more often — i.e. capital velocity, not a steady grind, is the bullish path shape. This contradicts the "steady grind continues" hypothesis for the graduation label. (2) Coordinated sniper cohorts raise first-30-min non-cohort buyer count by only +16.1% [+13.0, +19.4] after contamination adjustment and PSM, down from a naive +130.9% — so "smart wallets bought it" is nearly worthless once de-contaminated. (3) A published precision audit of a production Solana filter stack (2607.02830) shows early-death classification does NOT identify elevated rug risk (48.9% gone vs 57.6% gone for other rejects), so "it died fast" filters are not saving you from rugs. (4) Telegram presence in launch metadata carries Cox HR 5.402 for graduation and an 8.94x raw lift — it is a free metadata field you may not be using. (5) Cross-venue transfer of these models collapses (MCC -0.0026, AUCPRC 0.577 for Raydium->PumpFun), so a model trained on one launchpad will not carry.

On labelling and validation: chronological split by mint timestamp with no shuffling across the boundary, PR-AUC/MCC as primary metrics (never accuracy), scale_pos_weight = N_neg/N_pos, and a positive-class redefinition to lift the base rate — one paper raised PR-AUC 0.08 -> 0.40 purely by broadening a 2% target to a 6.1% target. Gradient boosting is at the ceiling; MELT's best deep model beats XGBoost by ~0.006 AUPRC.

## Numeric parameters

- **sniper_window_seconds** = `{0, 1, 5, 10} seconds after mint_ts` — MELT computes sniper_{0,1,5,10}s cohorts as any trader executing a swap at timestamp <= mint_ts + X. Four nested windows, three features each (num, ratio, hold_pct) = 12 features. Second-granularity because Solana block time makes finer resolution unreliable from RPC timestamps.
- **sniper_window_blocks (alternative)** = `K = 5 blocks after the creation block; bundle = the creation block itself` — arXiv 2601.08641 Algorithm 1 (bundle: non-creator buy in the creation block) and Algorithm 2 (sniper: non-creator buy within first K blocks, default K=5, ~2s). Use when you have slot numbers rather than wall-clock timestamps.
- **first_buyer_cohort_sizes** = `K in {1, 5, 10, 20} distinct first buyers` — MELT computes, for the first K distinct buying entities: initial_hold_pct (their first buy size / supply), hold_pct (current balance / supply), and hold_ratio = hold_pct/initial_hold_pct. hold_ratio below 1 means the launch cohort is already selling.
- **holder_concentration_cuts** = `top {1, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100} plus Gini over holders with balance >= 1 unit` — MELT Group 2 and Group 4. Also top10_to_top100 = top10_pct/(top100_pct+1e-6) as a shape statistic. Independent flag threshold from arXiv 2507.01963: top holders above 30% of supply.
- **concealment_gap** = `topK_pct_delta = cluster_level_topK_pct - address_level_topK_pct, K in {1,5,10,20,50,100}` — MELT Group 4. The population mean gap is large: address-level concentration understates entity concentration by roughly the difference between 28.13% of holders and 36.50% of supply being coordinated.
- **coordinated_supply_share_by_channel** = `co-purchase 6.46% holders / 9.16% supply; fund-flow 22.57% / 28.22%; Jito bundle 10.39% / 15.96%; union 28.13% / 36.50%` — MELT measured on 41k+ Solana launches. Build fund-flow first: it recovers 3.5x more coordinated supply than co-purchase for the same effort.
- **wash_trade_definition_intra_tx** = `buy and sell of the same token within a SINGLE transaction; 21.4% of 30.8M pre-migration transactions` — MELT 'zero' record type. Cheapest possible wash filter: one pass over the swap log, no cross-transaction state.
- **wash_trade_screen_daily** = `volume increase > 500% vs previous period AND |price change| < 5%` — arXiv 2507.01963 phase-1 screen. Scale the period down to your window length (e.g. compare the last 60s to the prior 60s) and treat it as a red flag rather than a proof.
- **wash_trade_zero_risk_tolerance** = `2% difference allowed between a maker's buy and sell volume` — arXiv 2507.01963, to absorb slippage and fees when identifying round-trip positions.
- **wash_trade_circular_volume** = `>= 99% of period volume generated by makers who both bought and sold in the same period` — arXiv 2507.01963 confirmation test. Strict by design — it is a specificity filter, not a sensitivity one.
- **LPI_price_inflation_test** = `phase 1: price up > 100% in 24h AND (volume growth <= 20% OR absolute volume < $1,000); phase 2: buy volume >= 90% of period volume AND <= 10 unique trading entities` — arXiv 2507.01963. Median capital to execute one was $54 and 50% produced >500% price increases — a vertical spike on tiny volume with <=10 entities is almost certainly manufactured.
- **volume_padding_index** = `W = mean(inter-trade interval) / (std(inter-trade interval) + eps); flag W > 10` — arXiv 2608.01609. Machine-regular spacing. Companion tests: CV of intervals < 0.3, lag-1 autocorrelation rho_1 > 0.9, and (P_max/P_min)/sqrt(T) > 5.
- **burst_ratio and route_repeat_ratio** = `burst_ratio = peak-bucket trade count / median-bucket trade count; route_repeat_ratio = repeated (from,to) pairs / tx_count` — arXiv 2603.13830. Ablation shows the trade-level block (these two plus interval stats) is worth delta PR-AUC -0.1843 when removed, vs -0.0573 for the address-level block.
- **dump_detector** = `tau^D = inf{t : r_t < -k * sigma_MAD}, k = 4, sigma_MAD scaled by 1/0.67449` — Marino et al. Identical to what you already run. But 92.22% of tokens with >= 30 swaps trip it at least once, so use time-to-first-trip and trips-per-minute, not the binary.
- **pump_and_dump_holder_collapse** = `tau_down = 0.73 on total holder reduction, with monotone decline in holder count and pool token balance` — arXiv 2603.24625, chosen as the largest value in a 0.50-1.00 stability sweep that still admits gradual variants. Their activity floor is < 5 transactions/hour on a trailing 24h average.
- **time_series_encoding** = `10-second OHLCV bars, MAX_T = 360 bars = 60 minutes, keep the LAST 360, ffill close, fill O/H/L from close, fill volume with 0` — MELT src/feat_gen.py. Price channels 0-3 share one global mean/std fit on train only; volume channel 4 uses a separate StandardScaler.
- **modelling_sample_filter** = `time_span_valid >= 60 seconds AND holder_num >= 100` — MELT dataset.py filter_valid. Launches below this are excluded from modelling entirely. Your 60-300s observation window straddles the time floor; check what fraction of your census clears the 100-holder floor.
- **class_imbalance_weight** = `scale_pos_weight = N_neg / N_pos computed WITHIN each training fold` — Used identically by MELT (pos_weight in BCEWithLogitsLoss and XGB scale_pos_weight) and arXiv 2607.17555. Global computation leaks fold composition.
- **minimum_positives_per_validation_fold** = `>= 5 positive samples; expand the validation window automatically until satisfied` — arXiv 2608.20271. Essential at your 1.3%-ever-5x rate: a 500-launch fold expects 6.5 positives, so many folds would otherwise have zero.
- **train_test_split** = `sort by mint timestamp, first 70% train / last 30% test, shuffle ONLY within train, last 10% of train as the early-stopping validation set` — MELT dataset.py load_dataset. Plus arXiv 2608.20271's stricter variant: forward rolling window using only the most recent 3 months for training, final window used once.
- **target_base_rate_floor** = `design the primary label to have >= 6% positives; 2% gives PR-AUC ~0.08 against a ~0.06 random baseline` — arXiv 2607.17555 measured exactly this: broadening 2% -> 6.10% moved PR-AUC 0.08 -> 0.40 with an unchanged feature set and model.
- **MELT_drawdown_label** = `min_ratio = min(price over 20 min post-migration) / migration price; high if < 0.3 or manipulated; low if >= 0.7 and not manipulated` — Distribution over 41,470 launches: 84.13% high / 11.34% medium / 4.53% low.
- **MELT_return_ratio_distribution** = `median -0.684; p25 -0.865; p75 -0.206; p90 +0.768; p95 +1.902; p99 +9.303; p99.9 +54.84; 19.70% > 0; 8.47% > +100%` — Computed by me from data/label/label.csv, n=41,470 MIGRATED tokens. Useful cross-check: 8.47% ever-2x on graduated tokens vs your 7.5% ever-2x on all launches — graduation buys you almost nothing on the upside tail.
- **wallet_informativeness_score** = `notional-weighted mean markout over a frozen 10-day window, >= 100 orders minimum, top decile as the cohort; expect Spearman 0.52 between adjacent windows` — arXiv 2608.04373. The 0.52 persistence sets your refresh cadence: weekly at most, and expect roughly half the ranking to churn.
- **regime_scalars** = `launches/day, trailing 7-day migration rate, SOL 24h return and 7-day realised vol; retrain tripwire at a 2x move in migration rate` — Observed cross-regime move: 0.63% (Sept-Oct 2025) -> 0.198% fast-regime (June 2026), authors' own ratio 3.18x, stated as an upper bound. Launch volume: ~21,859/day (2025) vs ~26,313/day (2026) vs 69,046 peak (Q4 2024).
- **filter_stack_realistic_miss_rate** = `112 misses out of 2,402 rejection events = 4.7%; conservative save-to-miss 3.7:1` — arXiv 2607.02830. Benchmark for how aggressive your own filter stack should be — a well-tuned stack forgoes about 1 winner per 21 rejections.

## Traps and failure modes

- The biggest one, and it is my own computation rather than a paper's claim: MELT's published predictions lift the risk label 3.5x (P@100 0.243 -> 0.85) while leaving the upside untouched (fraction of top-100 with return_ratio > 1 is 0.08-0.13 against a base rate of 0.0815; xgb's top-100 is 0.080, i.e. BELOW base). A high-risk classifier is a drawdown tool, not a hit-rate tool. If you wire it as an entry gate and measure success by 2x rate, you will conclude it works when it does not, because the 2x rate on the gated set will move around by several points on n=100 out of pure noise.
- Contamination in any 'good wallets bought it' feature. arXiv 2607.02795 shows the naive contrast is +130.9% and roughly half of that is arithmetic — the tracked wallets' own buys are in the outcome. After excluding them it is +63.9%, and after propensity matching on launch quality it is +16.1% on buyer count and statistically zero on SOL inflow. Their activity-matched placebo produced a LARGER effect than the real one in 100/100 seeds. Any wallet-cohort feature must exclude the cohort's own flow from the outcome and must be matched on launch quality, or it is measuring itself.
- Cross-venue and cross-curve transfer fails outright. Raydium-trained -> PumpFun-tested gives MCC -0.0026 and AUCPRC 0.5771 (chance). Do not pool pump.fun, bonk.fun, Meteora and Raydium launches into one training set without an explicit transfer test, and do not assume a model trained before a launchpad's curve/fee change survives it.
- 'It died fast' is not a rug-risk signal. arXiv 2607.02830's matched test: early-death-classified mints reach 'gone' at 48.9% vs 57.6% for other rejected mints — the wrong direction. Any filter you justify on early-death grounds needs a different justification.
- The 4-sigma dump detector fires on 92.22% of tokens with >= 30 swaps. As a binary entry feature it carries almost no information. Only the timing and frequency do.
- Feature-importance rankings dominated by has_twitter / has_telegram / has_website (arXiv 2608.01609) are usually describing the label source, not the market. That paper's labels come from a curated abuse-report feed, which is itself correlated with whether a project bothered to make a website. The Cox HR 5.402 for Telegram from arXiv 2607.02823 is more trustworthy because it comes from a prospectively collected launch feed with survival censoring — but it is also the most obviously spoofable field on the launch, so expect it to decay.
- Any paper reporting > 0.95 AUC on rug-pull prediction should be assumed leaked. arXiv 2603.11324 reports TabPFN at ROC-AUC 0.997 / PR-AUC 0.997 with 1 FN and 1 FP out of 200 on a hand-labelled n=1000 set. Compare against MELT's honest 0.58 AUPRC on 41k launches and Catching the Rug's 0.80 AUCPRC on 6.4M. The realistic ceiling on early prediction is PR-AUC 0.6-0.8 on a rug label and much lower on a return label.
- Random shuffling of train/test. MELT sorts strictly by mint_ts and shuffles only within the train portion. Because launches within a regime share market conditions, creators, and sometimes the same funding entity, a random split will leak and inflate everything. You additionally need a purge/embargo of at least your label horizon between the train boundary and the test start, which none of these papers implements explicitly.
- Building a classifier directly on a 1.3% (ever-5x) or 0.22% (ever-50x) target. At those rates PR-AUC is dominated by the base rate and gradient boosting will produce a near-constant score. arXiv 2607.17555 quantifies the fix: broadening 2% -> 6.10% moved PR-AUC from 0.08 to 0.40.
- Uncleaned volume features. With 21.4% of pre-migration transactions being intra-transaction wash trades and self-reported 'coordinated networks' accounting for whole tokens' volume, your volume imbalance and trade count are partly measuring a bot's own loop. Compute them on clean volume or they will point at the most heavily manufactured launches.
- Wallet-age computation via naive paging. getSignaturesForAddress returns newest-first with a 1,000 max; paging to the oldest signature on an active wallet is unbounded work. The bounded algorithm is: one call at limit=1000; if fewer than 1000 come back, the last element IS the wallet's first signature (exact age and funder); if exactly 1000 come back, the wallet is 'aged/active' and you stop. One RPC call per buyer, no paging, and the truncation case is itself the answer you want.
- CEX hot wallets collapsing your entity graph. MELT explicitly loads data/bundle/exchange_account.txt and drops any funding signer in it before building account2signer. Without this, every Coinbase-funded retail buyer merges into one giant 'cluster' and your concealment-gap features become garbage.
- Chainalysis-specific wash-trade parameters: I could not retrieve any Chainalysis page through this environment (their blog URLs 404). Every numeric wash-trade threshold I have given comes from a peer-reviewable arXiv source, not from Chainalysis. If someone quotes you a 'Chainalysis heuristic' with specific numbers, treat it as unverified marketing until it is read off their own publication.
- Search-engine access was exhausted in this session before I started (200/200 WebSearch calls already consumed), so all discovery went through the arXiv API, direct URL fetches, and a repo clone. Consequences: Kaggle competitions and non-arXiv GitHub work on this topic are UNSEARCHED, I found no dataset named 'MemeTrans' directly (it is referenced only as a ~40k-token prior baseline inside arXiv 2608.20271), and I could not retrieve the full text of arXiv 2607.02795 (sniper-cohort detection), so its exact co-occurrence edge threshold and its ten PSM covariate names remain unknown — those are in the RED-COHORT-2026-v1 Zenodo deposit at doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20978741 if you want them.
- Marino et al.'s 'fewer trades to reach a given vSol is better' result has no AUC, no coefficients, no standard errors and no out-of-sample validation — it is a set of non-parametric conditional-probability curves with 5-SOL bins. It contradicts the intuitive 'steady grind' hypothesis, which makes it interesting, but it is a hypothesis for your own census to adjudicate, not a finding to hard-code.

## Findings

### MELT ships the exact code for 122 features; the four groups and their windows are reproducible verbatim from src/feat_gen.py

Group 1 Context (6): SOL/USD price at the hour bucket ts-(ts%3600), migrate year/month/day/hour/weekday. Group 2 Holding concentration (59, address-level): holder_gini over holders with amount>=1; top{1,5,10,20,50,100}_pct of total supply; top10_to_top100 = top10_pct/(top100_pct+1e-6); sniper_{0,1,5,10}s_hold_pct, _num, _ratio (ratio = count/len(hold_df)); early_top{1,5,10,20}_initial_hold_pct (sum of the FIRST buy value of the first K distinct buyers / supply), early_top{K}_hold_pct (their CURRENT balance / supply), early_top{K}_hold_ratio = current/initial; dev_hold_pct, dev_initial_hold_pct, dev_hold_ratio (dev = signer of the mint&swap tx or of tx_list[1] when tx_list[0].type=='mint'); realized_pnl_mean and unrealized_pnl_mean for each of 12 cohorts {early_top5, early_top10, early_top20, sniper_0s, sniper_1s, sniper_5s, sniper_10s, top5, top10, top20, top100, all}. Group 3 Market activity (22): tx_num, tx_num_valid (swaps only), trader_tup_num, time_span (last_ts - mint_ts), time_span_valid, tx_per_sec, tx_per_sec_valid, wash_tx_num, wash_ratio, transfer_tx_num, transfer_ratio, trader_num, holder_num (balance>=1), buy_num, sell_num, buy_user_num, sell_user_num, buy_vol, sell_vol, avg_buy_vol, avg_sell_vol, sell_pressure = sell_vol/(buy_vol+1e-6). Group 4 Bundle/cluster (35): the whole of Group 2's concentration block recomputed on CLUSTER identities plus cluster_num, cluster_holder_num, cluster_holder_ratio, cluster_total_pct, cluster_avg_pct, cluster_max_pct, and six concealment deltas top{1,5,10,20,50,100}_pct_delta = group4_topK_pct - group2_topK_pct. Time series: 10-second OHLCV bars, MAX_T=360 bars (=60 minutes), left-padded/truncated to the LAST 360 bars, close forward-filled, open/high/low filled from close, volume filled 0.

**Actionable:** Add, in order of expected value: (1) early_top{1,5,10,20}_hold_ratio — first-buyer retention, a direct measure of whether the launch cohort is already unwinding; you have no analogue. (2) topK_pct_delta at K=10 and K=20 — the concealment gap between naive address concentration (which you already compute) and entity concentration. (3) sniper_{0,1,5,10}s_hold_pct. (4) sell_pressure and tx_per_sec_valid. (5) holder_gini instead of, or alongside, a top-N cut.

`confidence=high` · source: https://github.com/git-disl/MELT/blob/main/src/feat_gen.py

### MELT's entity resolution is a union-find over three edge types, and the fund-flow edge (same first-signer, CEX-excluded) is the single most productive one

Edges: (i) co-purchase — every trader appearing in the same transaction's trader_map when len(trader_map)>1 and type contains 'swap'; (ii) same Jito bundle — all traders in transactions sharing a bundle_id from bundle2sig_filtered.pkl; (iii) same funding signer — account2signer maps an account to the SIGNER OF ITS FIRST-EVER SIGNATURE (data/bundle/first_sig_signer.jsonl), with any signer present in data/bundle/exchange_account.txt dropped so that CEX hot wallets do not merge the world into one cluster. All three sets are pushed into cluster_set_list, then pairwise-intersected and unioned. Yields, per the paper: co-purchase 6.46% of holders / 9.16% of supply; fund-flow 22.57% / 28.22%; Jito bundle 10.39% / 15.96%; union 28.13% of holders holding 36.50% of supply. Of 30.8M pre-migration transactions, 21.4% are wash trades (their 'zero' type = buy and sell within a single transaction).

**Actionable:** Implement the fund-flow edge first — it is 3.5x more productive than co-purchase and does not require Jito bundle data. Per buyer wallet W: getSignaturesForAddress(W, limit=1000); if fewer than 1000 returned, the last element is W's first-ever signature — fetch that transaction, take its fee payer, that is W's funder, and its blockTime is W's exact age. If 1000 are returned, W has >=1000 lifetime signatures: classify as 'aged/active' and stop (one call, no paging). Maintain a local CEX-signer denylist so Coinbase/Binance/Kraken hot wallets do not collapse unrelated buyers into one entity.

### MELT's own shipped predictions show risk-classification lift does NOT translate into upside lift
