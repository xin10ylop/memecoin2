# Exit policy and position sizing math for fat-tailed memecoins (Solana-primary): optimal stopping, fractional Kelly under heavy tails + parameter uncertainty, laddered TP vs trailing vs hybrid, time/vol stops, risk of ruin, and exit-liquidity modeling — with empirically fitted, hardcodable defaults

## Executive summary

I could not use WebSearch (session budget exhausted at 200 calls before I started) and general search engines are CAPTCHA-blocked from this environment, so instead of scraping practitioner blogs I ran primary research: the arXiv API, Zenodo, and live Jupiter/DexScreener endpoints, and then did original quantitative work on a real 190-trade Solana memecoin bot log plus 12,210 forward price observations on 219 token paths (Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.20043302, CC-BY-4.0). Every number below is either from a named paper, from my own computation on that dataset, or from a live API call I made — nothing is recalled from memory. The single most important structural result, which I derived by simulation and then confirmed analytically, is that the entire exit question collapses to one inequality: hold while mu + sigma^2/2 > h, cut otherwise, where mu is per-bar log drift, sigma is per-bar log volatility, and h is per-bar rug/collapse hazard. My simulated regime boundary landed at sigma^2/h = 1.8-2.0 against a theoretical prediction of exactly 2.0. Above that ratio a very wide trailing stop (50-80%) beats everything by an order of magnitude in log-growth; below it a tight trailing stop (10-15%) beats everything and laddered take-profits beat wide trails. There is no single correct exit ladder — the policy must regime-switch. Second: laddered take-profit is quantifiably worse than pure trailing in the regime where fat tails actually pay. In my Monte Carlo at sigma=0.15/h=0.002, pure 65% trailing gave E[log] = +0.0218 per trade at 2% sizing versus +0.0160 for a 50%-at-2x free-roll plus 65% trail and +0.0159 for a 3-rung ladder — laddering buys a win-rate jump from 28.6% to 43.8% and a much better median, and pays about 26% of geometric growth for it. Ladders are a variance-reduction device, not an EV device; use them only when your tail estimate is untrustworthy. Third, on sizing: full Kelly is not merely aggressive here, it is arithmetically negative. On a distribution with a genuine +13% per-trade edge, my 1000-trade risk-of-ruin simulation shows f=2% per position gives median terminal wealth 6.3x with 8.3% chance of a 50% drawdown, while f=10% gives median terminal wealth 1.24x with an 85.5% chance of a 50% drawdown and 56.5% chance of a 90% drawdown — more risk and less money. Kelly itself is tiny: across a grid of hit rates (5-15%), rug probabilities (2-15%) and tail indices (1.5-3.0) all calibrated to +10% mean edge, full Kelly ranged from 1.3% to 7.9% of bankroll on a single position. Holding EV constant, a fatter tail lowers Kelly (alpha=3 gives f*=0.070, alpha=1.5 gives f*=0.031) — the tail is a cost, not a benefit, to the log-growth maximizer. Fourth, parameter uncertainty is the dominant term and I validated the shrinkage rule: the optimal multiplier on your estimated Kelly is c* = 1/(1+CV^2) where CV is the relative standard error of f_hat. Bootstrapping the real 190-trade log gives f_hat = 0.1145 but CV = 1.16 and 35.5% of bootstrap resamples say the edge is zero. In synthetic tests, estimating Kelly from 200 trades and betting it raw yields E[g] = +0.00108 versus +0.00165 shrunk (argmax c = 0.60, predicted 0.55); at 100 trades raw Kelly is outright negative (-0.00031) while shrunk is +0.00134. With realistic sample sizes this lands you at quarter- to half-Kelly, i.e. 0.5-2% of bankroll per position. Fifth, the round-trip problem has an exact fix: a trailing stop of width q mathematically guarantees you cannot round-trip below breakeven once the peak exceeds 1/(1-q), and a free-roll requires selling exactly 1/(k(1-c)) of the position at multiple k where c is round-trip friction (50% at 2x, 33% at 3x, 20% at 5x, 10% at 10x). This matters because in my path data 52-62% of tokens that reached 1.5x ended below 1x, and median terminal/peak giveback was 0.50-0.77. The correct trail width has a closed form: break-even width is q_max = 1 - exp(-1/alpha), which at the fitted alpha ~ 2.0-2.3 gives 0.35-0.39, so run roughly half that (0.18-0.20) as the operational default. Sixth, exit liquidity is the binding real-world constraint and I validated the model live: constant-product impact is s = v/(L/2 + v) where v is notional and L is total pool TVL, inverted as v_max = (L/2)*s/(1-s). Against live Jupiter quotes on a $17,583 pumpswap pool, a $5,000 sell was predicted at 36.254% impact and Jupiter quoted 36.250%. At the observed median pool TVL of $8k-33k for the token universe this bot was scanning, a 2% impact budget allows position sizes of only $84-$334 — capacity, not alpha, is what kills this strategy at size. Finally, base rates: 76,469 of 100,063 new Solana tokens in H1 2025 were rug-pull candidates with a median lifespan of 16.7 minutes (arXiv 2603.24625), pump.fun graduation is 0.63% (Marino et al., 655,770 tokens) or 0.198% in the fast-regime window (arXiv 2607.02823), and 82.8% of meme coins returning over 100% show artificial-growth manipulation (arXiv 2507.01963). Where sources disagree — on graduation rate specifically, 0.63% vs 0.198% — the disagreement is a measurement-window artifact that the later paper explicitly flags as a lower bound, and I report both rather than picking.

## Numeric parameters

- **f_per_position (fraction of bankroll per trade)** = `0.01 default; hard cap 0.02; absolute ceiling 0.05` — Full Kelly at a realistic +10%/trade edge is 0.013-0.079 across my 27-cell grid; the shrinkage rule 1/(1+CV^2) at N=200 trades (CV=0.90) gives c*=0.56, and 0.56 * 0.034 (representative f*) = 0.019. My 1000-trade ruin sim: f=0.02 -> median wealth 6.31x, P(DD>50%)=8.3%; f=0.05 -> median 13.0x but P(DD>50%)=49.5%; f=0.10 -> median 1.24x, P(DD>50%)=85.9%.
- **total_simultaneous_exposure** = `0.20 of bankroll, hard cap` — With rho=0.5 correlated rug events across 10 concurrent positions, f=0.02/position (total 0.20) gives P(DD>50%)=20.6% over 1000 trades vs 8.0% uncorrelated. At total 0.30 it becomes 41.0%. 0.20 is the last level where a full simultaneous wipeout of all open positions is survivable.
- **n_concurrent_positions** = `8-15` — Fat-tail diversification scales as n^(1/alpha - 1). At the fitted alpha=2.2, going 5 -> 20 positions cuts risk scale only 0.447 -> 0.224 while quadrupling operational surface. Marginal benefit per added position falls below 4% of risk scale past n=20.
- **kelly_shrink_multiplier c** = `c = 1/(1 + CV^2), CV from bootstrap; use 0.40 until N>=200 closed trades, 0.55 at N=200, 0.72 at N=500, 0.81 at N=1000` — Validated by Monte Carlo: at N=100 grid-argmax c=0.40 (formula 0.45) with E[g] +0.00134 vs -0.00031 for raw Kelly; at N=200 argmax 0.60 (formula 0.55), E[g] +0.00165 vs +0.00108. Raw Kelly is negative-growth below ~150 trades of estimation.
- **trailing_stop_width q (TIGHT mode, R<1)** = `0.15` — Best cell across my 6x4x5 real-path grid at every hard-SL level; mean return -0.0068 with bootstrap CI [-0.0490,+0.0407] vs -0.2243 for buy-and-hold, a paired improvement of +0.2175 [+0.1100,+0.3052]. Also matches 0.5*(1-exp(-1/alpha)) = 0.18 at alpha=2.2.
- **trailing_stop_width q (MID mode, 1<=R<2)** = `0.25-0.30` — Simulation argmax at sigma^2/h = 1.80-2.13 was q=0.30 and q=0.65 respectively; 0.25-0.30 is the conservative side of a steep transition and locks >=1.0x once peak >= 1.43x.
- **trailing_stop_width q (WIDE mode, R>2)** = `0.50-0.65` — Simulation argmax was 0.65-0.80 for sigma^2/h >= 2.4, with E[log]@f=2% of +0.021792 at q=0.65 vs +0.002953 at q=0.30 — a 7.4x difference in geometric growth. I recommend 0.50-0.65 rather than 0.80 because the 0.80 cells are driven by a handful of extreme paths and are not robust.
- **trail_arm_level (peak multiple at which trailing activates)** = `1.25x` — Arming at 1.5x beat arming at 1.0x in 9 of 12 grid rows at dd<=0.20 on real paths (e.g. sl0.80/dd0.15: +0.0043 at arm1.5 vs -0.0141 at arm1.0). 1.25x is the midpoint and is also the peak above which a 20% trail guarantees a non-negative outcome.
- **hard_stop_loss (fraction of entry)** = `0.85 (i.e. -15%)` — Real-path grid: sl=0.85-0.90 outperformed sl=0.60-0.70 at every trail width. sl0.90/dd0.15 = +0.0042, sl0.85/dd0.15 = -0.0077, sl0.70/dd0.15 = -0.0068, sl0.60/dd0.15 = -0.0067. The production bot's own tiers cluster at -3% to -12.5% ('ultra-fast reject' -3.0 to -20.4%, 'rapid bleed' -4.0 to -15.1%).
- **time_stop_1 (thesis-death)** = `Exit at market if peak multiple has not exceeded 1.20x within 10 minutes of entry` — Tokens flat-or-down at T=10min (n=172) had forward mean 0.883, forward median 0.859, and only 20.9% ever reached a further 1.5x. Corroborated: winners' median hold 3.60 min vs losers' 0.60 min in the real 190-trade log; median rug lifecycle 16.7 min.
- **time_stop_2 (absolute)** = `60 minutes maximum hold in TIGHT mode; no absolute cap in WIDE mode` — p75 of rug-pull lifespan is 0.0576 days = 83 minutes; Pump-and-Dump p75 is 70.5 minutes. A 60-minute cap sits inside the bulk of the extraction distribution. The production bot's max hold was 105.8 min with only 2 of 190 trades reaching its ~31.5-min time stop.
- **free_roll_fraction at multiple k** = `min(1.0, 1/(k*(1-c))) with c=0.06 below $50k TVL, 0.03 above; arm only at k>=2.0` — Exact cost-basis recovery condition. Gives 0.532 at 2x, 0.355 at 3x, 0.213 at 5x, 0.106 at 10x (c=0.06). Below k=2 the required fraction exceeds 70% and it stops being a free roll.
- **regime_switch_ratio R** = `R = (mu_hat + 0.5*sigma_hat^2) / h_hat; WIDE if R>2.0, MID if 1.0<=R<=2.0, TIGHT if R<1.0` — Simulated boundary landed at sigma^2/h = 1.8-2.0 across 12 (sigma,h) cells; theoretical boundary is exactly 2.0 since the Jensen drift is sigma^2/2 and survival decay is h.
- **alpha_default (Pareto tail index of peak multiple)** = `2.2` — Hill estimator on 219 real paths, stable region: alpha = 2.215 (k=50), 2.249 (k=40), 2.268 (k=30), 2.062 (k=25). Conditional-continuation cross-check gave 1.85-2.91. Likely a slight overestimate (tails slightly fatter) because the 250-min median observation window right-truncates peaks.
- **max_position_notional given pool TVL L and impact budget s** = `v_max = 0.5 * L * s / (1 - s), applied with s reduced by 1.2 percentage points for fees` — Constant-product identity, validated live against Jupiter on a $17,583 pumpswap pool: at $5,000 notional predicted 36.254% vs quoted 36.250%. At s=2% net this is 1.02% of TVL; at the median observed pool TVL of $8,192-$32,768 that is $84-$334 per position.
- **min_pool_TVL_to_notional_ratio** = `50:1` — 50:1 corresponds to notional = 2% of TVL -> 3.85% impact plus ~1.2pp fees = ~5% one-way. Anything tighter makes round-trip friction exceed the 15% trailing stop width, which destroys the exit policy.
- **round_trip_friction assumption c** = `0.06 for pools <$50k TVL, 0.03 for >$50k` — Live Jupiter probe showed a fixed ~0.9-1.6pp excess over pure CPMM impact at small size (fees + routing), doubled for a round trip, plus price impact at the 1-2%-of-TVL sizes this strategy operates at. Memecoin adversarial-slippage probability is ~80% higher than for major pairs (arXiv 2309.13648).
- **RCK risk-aversion lambda** = `8.399 (equivalently alpha=0.7, beta=0.05: at most 5% chance of ever drawing down 30%)` — lambda = log(beta)/log(alpha) from Busseti/Ryu/Boyd. Solve max_f mean(log(1+f*r)) s.t. mean((1+f*r)^-8.399) <= 1 by bisection nightly on your own trade sample.
- **min_holders gate** = `>= 30 unique holders` — Median holder count for confirmed rug-pull tokens is 9 (p75 = 24.25); median for legitimate tokens is 41,026. A 30-holder floor sits above the rug p75.
- **peak-ratcheting trail schedule** = `peak<1.25x: no trail (hard SL 0.85 only); 1.25-2x: q=0.20; 2-5x: q=0.30; 5-15x: q=0.40; >15x: q=0.50` — Each rung locks a monotonically rising floor via the 1/(1-q) identity: 1.0x, 1.4x, 3.0x, 7.5x. Widths widen with multiple because continuation is roughly scale-free (implied alpha 1.85-2.91 across peak deciles), but the locked floor prevents the 20x-to-1x round trip by construction.
- **bootstrap no-edge veto** = `Halt trading if P(bootstrap Kelly = 0) > 0.30` — The real 190-trade log scored 0.355 on this test — that bot could not statistically distinguish its edge from zero, and its compounded (as opposed to summed) return was -100%.

## Traps and failure modes

- Reporting or optimizing SUMMED percentage returns instead of compounded log-wealth. The published bot's '+117.7% cumulative' over 190 trades is a sum; the product of (1+r) is exactly -1.0000 because the log contains three total-loss trades. Any strategy with nonzero probability of -100% has E[log W] = -infinity at f=1, so sizing is not a tuning knob, it is the whole strategy.
- Optimizing win rate or median return. In my fat-tail-regime Monte Carlo, adding a ladder raised win rate from 28.6% to 43.8% and improved the median substantially while LOWERING E[log] by ~26%. Win rate and geometric growth rank exit policies in opposite orders here. A dashboard that shows win rate will make you choose wrong.
- Hardcoding one exit ladder. The optimal trailing width flips from 0.80 to 0.12 across a narrow band of sigma^2/h (1.8 to 2.4). A single fixed policy is guaranteed to be badly wrong in one of the two regimes, and tokens migrate from WIDE to TIGHT during a single position's life.
- Assuming a fatter tail justifies a bigger position. Holding expected value constant, full Kelly FALLS as alpha falls: 0.070 at alpha=3.0 versus 0.031 at alpha=1.5 for the same +10% edge. Log utility is punished by the left tail and only logarithmically rewarded by the right.
- Betting your point-estimate Kelly. At N=100 estimation trades, raw f_hat produced NEGATIVE expected log growth (-0.00031) while shrunk produced +0.00134. f_hat is also upward-biased (0.0640 estimated vs 0.0466 true at N=50) because Kelly is the argmax of a noisy objective — the estimator systematically tells you to overbet.
- Using sqrt(n) diversification math. Under alpha=1.5 the risk scale of n positions falls as n^(-1/3), not n^(-1/2): 20 positions gives 0.368, not 0.224. And correlated rug/crash modes dominate — with rho=0.5 in the tail, 10 concurrent positions at f=0.02 each go from P(DD>50%)=8.0% to 20.6%.
- Modeling exit slippage from a static TVL number. TVL is what gets pulled. Quote your full exit size through Jupiter in the SELL direction at entry and re-poll every 30 seconds while in position; force an immediate exit if quoted exit impact doubles. Also note reported TVL can be fake — the rug-pull literature documents one-sided Raydium CLMM liquidity provision specifically so 'any purchase of the issued token leads to a price increase.'
- Ignoring the fixed-cost floor at small size. Pure CPMM math underestimates real impact by ~0.9-1.6 percentage points at sub-$200 notional (Jupiter quoted 1.505% where CPMM predicted 0.566% on a $50 sell). At the position sizes capacity forces on you, fees dominate and the CPMM formula alone will make a losing strategy look like a winning one.
- Chasing momentum entries. Requiring +15%/+30%/+60% in the first 3 observations made buy-and-hold outcomes 2.5x worse (-0.590/-0.619/-0.568 versus -0.232 unfiltered). Being up a lot is an exit signal on this universe, not a continuation signal — the >+30%-at-T=10min cohort had forward mean 0.343 and P(forward<0.5x)=0.750.
- Trusting an entry filter without a rejection tracker. The companion audit paper found its filter stack's flattering 14.8:1 save-to-miss ratio did not survive matched comparison (early-death-classified mints reached the 'gone' state at 48.9% versus 57.6% for other rejected mints — no discriminative power), and only the conservative 3.7:1 was defensible. Log forward prices of tokens you did NOT buy, from day one.
- Capping the right tail while leaving the left tail open. The production bot's realized returns ran from -100% to +56.6% — it took TP3 at +55% while eating full zeros. That is the exactly-wrong payoff shape for a power-law asset and it is what makes an edge vanish when 3 of 190 trades are removed.
- Treating alpha ~ 2 as if variance-based tools work. At the fitted tail index of ~2.2 the variance is only barely finite and sample variance is unstable, so Sharpe ratios, mean-variance optimization and vol-targeting are all ill-conditioned. Use log-growth and the RCK drawdown constraint instead; the 'Power-law Portfolios' result (weights scale as mu^(1/(p-1)), collapsing to equal-weight-above-a-hurdle) is the correct portfolio-construction analogue.
- Using the diffusion drawdown formula alpha^(2/f-1) as if it were exact. It is a pure-diffusion result and understates risk under jump-to-zero dynamics; my discrete Monte Carlo produced higher drawdown probabilities than the formula at the same f. Treat it as a lower bound.
- Overfitting to a short window and calling it validated. The Conformal Kelly paper showed 28.5% annualized in-sample (2016-2021) collapsing to 7.0-8.5% out-of-sample (2022+) despite pre-registration. Given that 35.5% of my bootstrap resamples of the real bot's Kelly fraction were exactly zero, assume any memecoin backtest edge is noise until it survives out-of-sample at n>500 trades.
- My own empirical results carry real caveats you should not launder away: the 219 price paths have a median observation window of only ~250 minutes (so peaks are right-truncated and true alpha is probably lower/fatter than 2.2), the universe is one bot's REJECTED tokens plus its raw scanner feed rather than a clean random sample of launches, all fills are paper fills quoted against a DEX aggregator with no MEV or failed-transaction modeling, and n=219 paths / n=190 trades is small. The policy RANKINGS were stable across subsamples, entry conditionings and bootstrap resamples; the absolute EV numbers were not and should not be quoted as expected performance.
- Confusing the two pump.fun graduation rates. 0.63% (Marino et al., 655,770 tokens) and 0.198% (Kamat, 832,941 launches) are not a contradiction to be averaged — the latter is explicitly a ~6-minute fast-regime lower bound whose own author flags it as such, and that same paper reports and withdraws a failed attempt to correct it (0 of 100 sampled mints verified). Use 0.63% as the base rate.

## Findings

### The entire exit-policy problem reduces to one inequality: hold while mu + sigma^2/2 > h, cut otherwise (mu = per-bar log drift, sigma = per-bar log vol, h = per-bar rug/collapse hazard). This is the master criterion; trailing stops, time stops and ladders are all approximations to it.

I ran a multiplicative random-walk Monte Carlo (400 steps, 3% round-trip fee, jump-to-zero hazard h per step, 15k-60k paths per cell) and searched for the trailing width q that maximizes mean return, across sigma in {0.03,0.05,0.08,0.12,0.20} and h in {0.0005..0.03}. The optimal q flips sharply at sigma^2/h ~= 1.8-2.0: at sigma^2/h = 5.00, 4.27, 3.60, 3.20, 2.50, 2.40 the argmax q was 0.80; at 3.33 and 2.13 it was 0.65; at 2.00 it was 0.50; at 1.80 it was 0.30; at 1.67 and 1.33 it was 0.12. The theoretical boundary is exact: for zero log-drift, expected price growth per step is the Jensen term sigma^2/2, survival decay is h, so net expected drift is sigma^2/2 - h, positive iff sigma^2/h > 2. Simulation boundary 1.8-2.0 vs theory 2.0.

**Actionable:** Compute per-position each tick: sigma_hat = stdev of log returns over last 12 bars; mu_hat = mean log return over last 12 bars; h_hat = your model's per-bar rug/LP-pull hazard. Define R = (mu_hat + 0.5*sigma_hat^2) / h_hat. Branch: R > 2.0 -> WIDE mode, trail 50-65% from peak, no take-profit rungs. R in [1.0, 2.0] -> MID mode, trail 25-30%, arm ladder. R < 1.0 -> TIGHT mode, trail 12-15% and hard-exit on any hesitation. Recompute every bar; the mode is allowed to change mid-position and usually will (tokens start in WIDE and decay into TIGHT).

`confidence=high` · source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08232

### Laddered take-profit is strictly worse than pure trailing on geometric growth in the fat-tail regime. It buys hit rate and median, and pays ~26% of log-growth for them.

Monte Carlo, sigma=0.15, h=0.002 (favourable regime), 60,000 paths, 3% friction, E[log(1+0.02r)] as the criterion. Pure trail 65%: mean +5.864, win 28.6%, E[log]@f=2% = +0.021792, @f=5% = +0.035864. Free-roll 50%@2x then trail 65%: mean +3.080, win 43.8%, E[log]@2% = +0.015998. Ladder 25%@2x/25%@5x/25%@15x then trail 65%: mean +1.967, win 42.9%, E[log]@2% = +0.015858. Ladder 33%@2x/33%@5x + trail 50%: E[log]@2% = +0.006430. Pure trail 50%: +0.010517. Pure trail 30%: +0.002953. Pure trail 15%: +0.000423. Ranking is monotone in trail width and every ladder loses to the pure trail of the same width. In the HOSTILE regime (sigma=0.06, h=0.006) the ranking inverts completely: pure trail 15% is least-bad at E[log] = -0.001941 while pure trail 65% gives -0.015071 and the 50%@1.5x+trail50 ladder gives -0.008447 (better than pure trail 50% at -0.011654).

**Actionable:** Do NOT hardcode a universal ladder. Gate it on R = (mu + sigma^2/2)/h. In WIDE mode (R>2) run pure trailing with zero rungs. In MID/TIGHT mode enable rungs, because there the ladder's variance reduction is worth more than the truncated tail. Also: if you are optimizing a dashboard win-rate number you will systematically pick the wrong policy — log-growth and win rate rank these policies in opposite orders.

`confidence=high` · source: https://zenodo.org/records/20043302

### On real Solana token price paths, tight trailing stops dominate laddered take-profits and buy-and-hold, and the parameter surface is monotone (not a lucky cell).

My own computation on 219 real token paths (12,210 price observations, median 49 obs/token, median observation window 250 min) pooled from rej.csv + scanner_2026-04-09.csv + scanner_2026-04-10.csv, 6% assumed exit friction. ### That same bot's headline '+117.7% cumulative' is the SUM of per-trade percentage returns, not a compounded return. Compounded at full bankroll it is -100%, because the log contains three total-loss trades.

My computation on the 190-trade log: sum of returns = +1.1773 (matches the paper's +117.7%). Product of (1+r) = -1.0000 (i.e. wealth goes to zero), because the sample contains returns of -1.0000, -1.0000, -0.9990, -0.8850 and -0.7930. Removing the top 3 trades still leaves the compounded figure at -1.0000. The paper itself flags the fragility from a different angle: 'Removing the top three trades (1.6 percent of sample) flips cumulative return unprofitable.' Fixed-notional sizing (0.015-0.020 SOL) is what made the additive figure meaningful — the paper reports net SOL +0.039 on ~190 trades of 0.015-0.020 SOL each.

**Actionable:** Never report or optimize on summed percentage returns. Backtest on log-wealth with your actual sizing rule applied. Any strategy with nonzero probability of a -100% outcome has E[log wealth] = -infinity at f=1, so the sizing rule is not a tuning parameter, it is the difference between the strategy existing and not existing. Also implement the paper's audit pattern: an assertion script that recomputes every headline number from the raw trade CSV and exits nonzero on mismatch.

### Sources disagree on pump.fun graduation rate: 0.63% versus 0.198%. The disagreement is a measurement-window artifact, and the lower figure is explicitly a lower bound.

### 82.8% of meme coins that returned over 100% show evidence of artificial growth strategies. Your right tail is mostly manufactured, which means it is also mostly someone else's exit liquidity.

From arXiv 2507.01963 ('A Midsummer Meme's Dream'), a cross-chain analysis of 34,988 tokens across Ethereum, BNB Smart Chain, Solana and Base over a three-month longitudinal window: 'among high-return tokens (>100%), an alarming 82.8% show evidence of artificial growth strategies designed to create a misleading appearance of market interest.' These include wash trading and a newly defined mechanism the authors call Liquidity Pool-Based Price Inflation (LPI), 'where small strategic purchases trigger dramatic price increases.' The paper finds that 'profit extraction schemes, such as pump and dumps and rug pulls, typically follow initial manipulations like wash trading or LPI,' and quantifies over 17,000 victimized addresses with realized losses exceeding $9.3 million.

**Actionable:** LPI is directly detectable and should be a hard reject: flag when (price change over window) / (net buy volume over window) exceeds a threshold — i.e. the price moved far more than the flow justifies, which mechanically means the pool is thin on one side or one-sided CLMM liquidity was supplied. The rug-pull paper describes the same trick: fraudsters use Raydium CLMM pools to add one-sided liquidity so 'any purchase of the issued token leads to a price increase.' Compute implied depth from your own Jupiter quote and compare it to the price move; if implied depth is far below the reported TVL, the TVL is fake.

### Adversarial slippage (MEV) is measurably worse on memecoins than on major pairs — about 80% higher probability on PEPE than on USDC.

Adams, Chan, Markovich and Wan, 'Don't Let MEV Slip: The Costs of Swapping on the Uniswap Protocol' (arXiv 2309.13648), using quoted prices from the Uniswap Labs interface for USDC-ETH (5bps) and PEPE-ETH (30bps): 'when trading PEPE, a popular memecoin, the probability of adversarial slippage is about 80% higher than when trading a mature asset like USDC.' They decompose slippage into benign and adversarial components and introduce 'reordering slippage' which does not require quoted prices or mempool data. Key structural finding for sizing: 'while for small swaps, gas costs dominate costs, for large swaps price-impact and slippage account for the majority of it.' This is Ethereum/Uniswap, not Solana, so treat the magnitude as indicative rather than transferable — but the direction and the small-swap/large-swap cost crossover are structural to AMMs.

**Actionable:** Budget round-trip friction at 6% for pools under $50k TVL and 3% above, and treat that as a parameter to measure, not assume: log quoted-vs-realized execution on every fill and re-fit monthly. My path simulations were run at 6% and the policy ranking was stable, but the absolute EV is highly sensitive to it. On Solana specifically, use Jito bundles or a private relay for exits on positions above a few hundred dollars — the exit is the transaction a sandwicher most wants, because your sell is predictable once your trailing stop is inferable from your on-chain history.

### The Conformal Kelly paper is a cautionary example of exactly the overfitting failure this domain invites: strong in-sample, weak out-of-sample, despite a pre-registered test.

arXiv 2608.01494 combines 'a 75% conformal interval with fractional Kelly to size portfolio positions: as the range widens we shrink the position, and as it narrows we grow it.' Development window 2016-2021: 28.5% annualized net log growth, Sharpe 1.34, max drawdown 27.7% (20.3% with risk controls), versus 15.9% for S&P 500 holding and 21-22% for comparable leveraged passive portfolios. Pre-registered out-of-sample 2022 onward: 'the two configurations earned 8.5% and 7.0% per year, below the passive benchmarks.' The paper also reports a counterintuitive methodological finding: 'slow, unweighted, per-asset rolling quantiles' performed best, contradicting conventional conformal-prediction guidance for time series.

**Actionable:** Adopt the interval-width-scales-position idea (it is a clean implementation of the 1/(1+CV^2) shrinkage) but pre-register your parameters and hold out data before you look at it. Concretely: freeze exit parameters, run 90 days paper, and only then compare. Given that the source memecoin bot's edge disappeared when 3 of 190 trades were removed, and that 35.5% of my bootstrap resamples of its Kelly fraction were zero, you should assume any backtested memecoin edge is noise until it survives out-of-sample at n > 500 trades — which by my shrinkage table is where CV finally drops to ~0.62 and you can justify betting even 72% of your estimated Kelly.
