# Automated rug / honeypot / scam detection for Solana (and EVM) tokens — the SAFETY FILTER layer: third-party APIs (RugCheck, GoPlus, Birdeye, Honeypot.is, Quick Intel, TokenSniffer, SolSniffer, Solana Tracker, Trench) and native on-chain implementations (authorities, Token-2022 extensions, LP burn/lock, holder concentration, creator history, bundle/sniper detection, funding-graph clustering, wash-trade detection) with exact thresholds and RPC/API calls.

## Executive summary

I live-probed every API in scope rather than trusting docs. RugCheck's full report endpoint (`GET https://api.rugcheck.xyz/v1/tokens/{mint}/report`) is open, keyless, and returns the single richest safety payload available for free on Solana: parsed Token-2022 extensions, per-market LP lock %, top holders with owner + insider flag, `creatorTokens[]` (prior mints by the same creator, with market caps), a labelled `knownAccounts` map (AMM / LOCKER / CREATOR), and a `risks[]` array whose names and integer weights I harvested live — e.g. Mint Authority still enabled = 50000, Permanent Control Enabled (permanent delegate) = 50000, Freeze Authority = 25000, Creator history of rugged tokens = 88800–108000, Large Amount of LP Unlocked = 11000, High holder concentration (top-10 >50%) = 1001. `score_normalised` is 0–100 where HIGHER = RISKIER, and empirically ≈ 30·log10(raw_score) − 70 for raw ≳ 2000. Its keyless rate limit header reads `x-rate-limit-limit: 15` (per second, not per minute — I sustained 18 sequential calls without a 429). GoPlus is the best cross-chain second opinion: `GET https://api.gopluslabs.io/api/v1/solana/token_security?contract_addresses={mint}` and `GET .../api/v1/token_security/{chain_id}?contract_addresses={addr}` both answered unauthenticated, and 30 rapid unauthenticated calls returned 30×200. Honeypot.is (`GET https://api.honeypot.is/v2/IsHoneypot?address=&chainID=`) is keyless and is the only real buy/sell *simulation* in the set — mandatory for Base/BSC, useless for Solana. Three critical traps I verified: (1) RugCheck's `lpLockedPct` is 0 for every concentrated-liquidity market (Meteora DLMM, Meteora DAMM v2, Raydium CLMM) because there is no LP mint — the field returns `lpMint: "11111111111111111111111111111111"` — so a naive `lpLockedPct >= 90` gate rejects ~80% of current Solana launches as false positives; in my sample of 40 recent tokens, 90 of 160 markets were meteora_damm_v2 and 70 were meteoraDlmm. (2) RugCheck computes `graphInsidersDetected` and `insiderNetworks[]` but does NOT fold them into the score — I found a token with 8 insiders detected across two 4-wallet transfer networks and `score_normalised: 1`. You must gate on the insider graph yourself. (3) New pump.fun mints are now issued under Token-2022 (`TokenzQdBNbLqP5VEhdkAS6EPFLC1PHnBqCXEpPxuEb`), 9 of 10 in my live sample — any code branching on the legacy Token program ID silently skips the extension checks that matter most. On thresholds, practitioner consensus is tighter than the APIs' defaults: reject top-10 non-LP holders >25–30% (RugCheck only warns at >50%), dev/creator holding >5–10%, snipers >10%, bundled supply >20% (Trench's own guidance flags 3+ wallets holding meaningful supply; a 24-wallet/76% bundle is their canonical "extremely deliberate" example). Chainalysis's published wash-trade heuristics are directly implementable: one buy + one sell by the same address within 25 blocks with <1% volume difference, 3+ times, flagged 23,436 addresses and $704M in 2024; their pump-and-dump filter (deployer removes ≥65% of liquidity worth ≥$1,000, pool then dead 30 days) tagged 74,037 tokens = 3.59% of all 2024 launches, 94% rugged by the pool creator. Build the filter as a two-tier pipeline: a sub-100ms native RPC hard-gate (authorities, extensions, supply concentration from `getTokenLargestAccounts`) that never blocks on a third party, then an async enrichment tier (RugCheck + GoPlus + insider graph) that can veto before size-up but not before the first probe fill.

## Numeric parameters

- **mint_authority** = `must be null (revoked) — hard reject otherwise` — RugCheck weights 'Mint Authority still enabled' at 50000 (danger, its joint-highest non-creator weight); Solana Tracker weights it 2500. Detect via getAccountInfo jsonParsed .parsed.info.mintAuthority == null, or GoPlus mintable.status == '0'.
- **freeze_authority** = `must be null (revoked) — hard reject otherwise` — RugCheck 25000; Solana Tracker 7500 (their highest non-rugged danger weight). Freeze is the more lethal of the two for a bot because it blocks the exit, not just dilutes. Detect via .parsed.info.freezeAuthority == null or GoPlus freezable.status == '0'.
- **permanent_delegate** = `must be absent — hard reject` — RugCheck 'Permanent Control Enabled' = 50000, equal to mint authority. Delegate can burn or transfer any holder's balance with no signature, defeating all price-based stops.
- **transfer_hook** = `must be absent, or programId in an explicit allowlist — hard reject otherwise` — Arbitrary program executes on every transfer and can unconditionally revert sells. This is the canonical Token-2022 honeypot and no price or liquidity check detects it.
- **non_transferable / default_account_state==frozen / pausableConfig / confidentialTransferMint / mintCloseAuthority / scaledUiAmountConfig / interestBearingConfig** = `all must be absent — hard reject` — nonTransferable = unsellable. defaultAccountState frozen = buy succeeds, sell fails on a freshly created account. pausable = trading can be halted. confidentialTransfer = whale dumps invisible to your analytics. mintCloseAuthority = mint can be closed and re-initialised at the same address with different extensions, invalidating any cached verdict. scaledUiAmount/interestBearing = UI amount diverges from raw amount and corrupts position accounting.
- **transfer_fee_basis_points (Token-2022) / buy_tax + sell_tax (EVM)** = `reject if transferFeeConfig present at all for a scalping bot; if permitting, cap at 500 bps (5%) total round-trip` — Fee is deducted from the recipient, so quoted and received amounts diverge silently. GoPlus/honeypot.is express EVM taxes as decimals 0-1 where 1 = 100% or untradeable. Solana Tracker's Meteora dynamic-fee ladder starts penalising at >5% (score 1000) and reaches 7500 at >75%.
- **top10_non_lp_holder_pct** = `reject > 25% (default); sources bracket 15% / 25-30% / 50%` — Genuine disagreement: Solana Tracker scores top-10 >15% at 5000; terminal practitioners say >30% means 'likely bundled or dev sniped their own coin' and recommend a 25-30% cap; RugCheck only raises a warn at >50% ('The top 10 users hold more than 50% token supply', score 1001). Must exclude AMM vaults, LOCKER addresses, burn addresses and the bonding curve — use RugCheck knownAccounts type ∈ {AMM, LOCKER} or Birdeye's top10UserPercent which already excludes program accounts.
- **single_holder_pct (largest non-LP holder)** = `reject > 10%` — RugCheck fires 'Single holder ownership' at observed value 22.08% with score 2207 (warn) escalating to 5000-5005 (danger); Solana Tracker scores single holders on a ladder from >10% (2000) up to >90% (7000). 10% is the point where a single exit moves the price beyond typical slippage tolerance.
- **dev_creator_holding_pct** = `reject > 5%; 10% is the loosest defensible cap` — Terminal practitioner guidance: 'if the dev owns more than 5%, it's risky'; a recommended new-pair filter setting is 10% max. Track separately from generic top-10 concentration — Chainalysis found 94% of rugged pools were rugged by the pool creator, so creator holdings deserve a tighter bar than a random whale's.
- **sniper_supply_pct** = `reject > 10%; >15% is a documented high-risk indicator; >20% is a documented auto-reject` — Terminal guidance recommends 10% max to 'clean out farmed coins'. Solana Tracker documents snipers >15% as high-risk and >20% as a trade-gate rejection.
- **bundled_supply_pct** = `reject > 20%; 30%+ is documented as statistically correlated with rug probability` — Terminal guidance: 20% max 'to clear out obvious bad coins'. DeFade: insiders controlling 20-50% from block zero = high manipulation risk; 30%+ held by bundled wallets correlates with rug. Solana Tracker documents bundlers >15% as a high-risk indicator. MemeTrans measured bundled accounts holding 36.50% of supply on average across 41,470 migrated tokens — i.e. the population mean is already above every one of these thresholds.
- **bundle_wallet_count_in_single_slot** = `flag at >= 3 wallets AND >= 2% still held; Axiom uses >= 4 transactions in the same block` — Trench: ignore 2-wallet low-% bundles, red-flag '3+ wallets holding larger amounts of supply'; their false-positive floor is stated as 'at most 1-2% per bundle'. Axiom Pulse defines the bundle signature as four or more transactions landing in the same block. Their canonical bad case is 24 wallets / 76% of supply in one 0.4s slot.
- **bundle_detection_slot_window** = `same slot (400ms), extended to slot0 .. slot0+2` — Solana slot time ~400ms with no global mempool, so same-slot buys from distinct wallets are effectively never organic. DeFade extends the coordination window to 'within 1-2 slots' of liquidity events.
- **typical_bundle_wallet_count** = `5-20 wallets per launch` — DeFade's stated typical range; useful as a prior when deciding whether a detected cluster is a bundle or noise.
- **fresh_wallet_age_threshold** = `first-ever transaction within 24h of launch = 'fresh'; reject if fresh_share of first-slot buyers > 0.7 AND their combined supply > 20%` — Bundle wallets are 'created hours or minutes before the launch' with no prior transaction history. Compute via getSignaturesForAddress walked to the oldest signature.
- **lp_locked_or_burned_pct** = `require >= 90% — but ONLY for markets with a real LP mint` — RugCheck fires 'Large Amount of LP Unlocked' at 11000 (danger) with observed value 100.00% unlocked; Solana Tracker scores 'LP not burned/locked' at 4000. CRITICAL EXCEPTION: skip this check entirely when markets[].lp.lpMint == '11111111111111111111111111111111', which is the case for every meteora_damm_v2, meteoraDlmm and raydium_clmm pool — 160 of 160 CL markets in my live sample returned lpLockedPct 0 with a null LP mint.
- **raydium_lp_burn_formula** = `maxLpSupply = max(actualSupply, lpReserve - 1); burnPct = (maxLpSupply - actualSupply) / maxLpSupply * 100; normalise both by 10^decimals first` — Shyft's documented Raydium method. lpReserve in the AmmInfo account is the historical high-water mark; comparing it to the live getTokenSupply on lpMint reveals burns.
- **lp_lock_expiry_horizon** = `treat LP as unlocked if the lock expires within your maximum holding period` — GoPlus /open/api/v1/locks/token returns endTime per lock. A 6-hour lock on a token you hold for 8 hours provides zero protection; burned LP has no expiry and is strictly safer than locked LP.
- **creator_prior_mint_count** = `reject if the creator has any prior mint whose liquidity is now ~0; treat >= 3 launches in 24h as a serial deployer` — RugCheck 'Creator history of rugged tokens' is weighted 88800-108000 — by far its heaviest risk, 1.8-2.2x mint authority. Live example: a creator with 6 prior mints, one created 4 minutes earlier. GoPlus EVM gives honeypot_with_same_creator as a direct count — reject on > 0.
- **min_liquidity_usd** = `reject below $5,000-$10,000 depending on your position size; RugCheck fires 'Low Liquidity' at observed values as low as $7.20` — RugCheck 'Low Liquidity' scores 56 (warn) to 2992 (danger); Solana Tracker: Very Low Liquidity 7500 danger, Low Liquidity 5000 warning. Practitioner new-pair floors are much lower (volume $500+, market cap $5,000+) because they are hunting sub-minute entries. Size the floor to your own worst-case exit slippage, not to a generic number.
- **wash_trade_heuristic_1 (matched buy-sell)** = `same address, one buy + one sell within 25 blocks (~5 min; ~750 Solana slots), volume difference < 1%, occurring 3+ times` — Chainalysis's published parameters. Flagged 23,436 addresses and $704M in 2024 = 0.035% of November 2024 DEX volume. Average flagged address: 2 pools, 129 trades, $30,033.
- **wash_trade_heuristic_2 (multi-sender)** = `a controller funds 5+ addresses via multi-sender, and buy-sell value difference within a single pool is < 5%` — Chainalysis's second published heuristic. $1.87B in 2024 = 0.046% of November DEX volume. Average controller managed 183 addresses; maximum 22,832.
- **wash_volume_share_reject** = `reject if flagged wash volume > 10% of total volume` — Baseline real wash trading is ~0.08% of aggregate DEX volume (Chainalysis $2.57B combined). A token at 10%+ is two orders of magnitude above base rate and is not a marginal call.
- **trades_per_unique_trader** = `flag above ~5; A-A overlap (LP address also appears as a swapper) is an instant reject` — Academic A-A definition: a swapper identical to one of the pool's liquidity providers is a self-trade by definition, requiring no clustering. On pump.fun this maps to the bonding-curve creator appearing among early buyers.
- **rug_label_definition (for backtests/training)** = `deployer removes >= 65% of liquidity valued at >= $1,000, pool then has no transactions for 30 days, and the pool previously had > 100 transactions` — Chainalysis's exact published filter. Applied to 2024: 74,037 tokens = 3.59% of 2,063,519 launched; 94% rugged by the pool creator; mean time to abandonment 6.23 days, median 0 days, p99 123 days.
- **alternative_rug_label (fast, memecoin-specific)** = `min_price_ratio < 0.3 within 20 minutes of migration` — MemeTrans's high-risk rule. Their low-risk rule is min_price_ratio >= 0.7 AND manipulation pred_score < 0.3. Under this labelling 84.13% of 41,470 migrated pump.fun tokens are high-risk and only 4.53% are low-risk.
- **honeypot_is_reject_bands** = `reject riskLevel >= 60 (bands: very_low 0, low 1-19, medium 20-59, high 60-79, very_high 80-89, honeypot 90-100); also reject if risk == 'unknown' or riskLevel is absent` — Documented bands. very_low (0) is reserved for whitelisted tokens only. An absent riskLevel means the simulation could not conclude — fail closed.
- **honeypot_is_holderAnalysis gates** = `reject if failed/holders > 0.05, or siphoned > 0, or highestTax > your sell-tax ceiling, or maxSell/maxBuy < 0.95` — holderAnalysis reports real historical outcomes across thousands of holders, catching per-address discriminatory taxes a single simulation misses. Verified clean baseline on PEPE: 13,665/13,666 successful, 0 siphoned, highestTax 0.
- **rugcheck_score_normalised gate** = `<= 35 for a permissive gate, <= 10 for conservative; a single danger authority flag lands around 60-64` — Empirical mapping norm ≈ 30·log10(raw) − 70 above raw ~2000, from live pairs (2208,30) (3001,34) (12001,53) (13206,54) (14493,55) (24601,63) (75101,76). Freeze authority alone = raw 25000 → ~62. Do arithmetic on the raw score, never the normalised one.
- **rugcheck_rate_limit** = `design for 10 req/s per IP (server header says x-rate-limit-limit: 15, per second)` — Verified live: header reads 15, and 18 sequential requests all returned 200 with remaining 13-14. FluxRPC docs claim 1/s, which contradicts observed behaviour — 10/s is the safe compromise.
- **birdeye_token_security_cost** = `25 CU per call; free Standard tier = 30,000 CU/month ≈ 1,200 calls/month; hard cap at 5× the included allotment` — Documented CU cost and tier. At 1,200 calls/month Birdeye cannot be your primary per-launch check — reserve it for tokens that already passed the free tiers and are candidates for real size.
- **pump_fun_structural_fingerprint** = `supply == 2000000000000000, decimals == 6, mintAuthority null, freezeAuthority null, tokenMeta.mutable false, updateAuthority == '11111111111111111111111111111111', extensions ⊆ {metadataPointer, tokenMetadata}` — Verified live on a clean pump.fun launch. Any deviation on a token claiming pump.fun origin is a spoof — reject without further analysis. Inverts the filter from enumerating infinite attacks to validating one known-good shape.
- **sniper_exit_timing (informs entry delay, not a filter)** = `11% of snipers exit within 15s, 55%+ within 60s, 85% within 5 minutes` — Pine Analytics. Entering at T+5min faces materially less insider sell pressure than T+10s; the sniper-percentage filter matters most for sub-minute entries and can be relaxed for later entries.
- **expected_ceiling_on_filter_value** = `a well-tuned ML filter cut losses from -60.71% to -26.64% on top-100 selections (56.1% reduction)` — MemeTrans, best model AUPRC 0.5729, high-risk precision 0.8459 / recall 0.8325 / F1 0.8391. The safety layer reduces bleed; it does not create edge. Size positions accordingly.

## Traps and failure modes

- LP-BURN FALSE POSITIVE (highest-impact bug in this domain). RugCheck's markets[].lp.lpLockedPct is 0 for every concentrated-liquidity pool because those pools have no LP mint — lp.lpMint returns '11111111111111111111111111111111'. In my live sample 165 of 176 markets were CL types (meteora_damm_v2 90, meteoraDlmm 70, raydium_clmm 5). A gate of `lpLockedPct >= 90` therefore rejects the overwhelming majority of current Solana launches as unlocked. Branch on marketType and on lpMint == system program BEFORE reading the field.
- TOKEN-2022 IS NOW THE DEFAULT ON PUMP.FUN. 9 of 10 mints in the live /v1/stats/new_tokens feed had program TokenzQdBNbLqP5VEhdkAS6EPFLC1PHnBqCXEpPxuEb. Code that only handles TokenkegQfeZyiNwAJbNbGKPFXCWuBvf9Ss623VQ5DA will skip the extension parsing entirely and pass honeypots. Conversely, code that treats 'is Token-2022' as itself a risk signal will reject almost every new launch. The signal is the extension SET, not the program id.
- RUGCHECK'S SCORE IGNORES ITS OWN INSIDER GRAPH. Verified: a token with graphInsidersDetected = 8 across two 4-wallet transfer networks returned score_normalised = 1 and risks = []. If your gate is `score_normalised <= N` you have no insider protection at all. Call /insiders/graph separately and gate on it yourself.
- GOPLUS RETURNS STRINGS, NOT BOOLEANS. mintable.status, freezable.status, is_honeypot, cannot_sell_all etc. are the strings '0' and '1'. `if (result.is_honeypot)` is TRUE for the string '0' in JavaScript and Python. Meanwhile trusted_token is an integer, so the typing is inconsistent within a single response object. Parse explicitly.
- GOPLUS CODE 2 IS A PARTIAL RESULT, NOT AN ERROR. It arrives as HTTP 200 with a well-formed but incomplete result — fields may be missing or default. Docs say retry in ~15 seconds. Accepting a code-2 body as a pass is a direct route to buying a honeypot. Always check code == 1 and fail closed otherwise.
- GOPLUS RESULT KEYS ARE LOWERCASED EVM ADDRESSES. I passed a checksummed Base address and the result key came back fully lowercase. Looking up by the original checksummed string yields undefined, which naive code may interpret as 'no risks found'.
- GOPLUS BATCH DOES NOT RELIABLY FAN OUT. I passed two comma-separated Base addresses and received exactly one key in result. Never assume every requested address is present; iterate the requested list and treat a missing key as 'unknown', not 'clean'.
- GOPLUS default_account_state == '1' DOES NOT MEAN FROZEN. It is the Token-2022 extension default meaning 'Initialized'. Frozen is '2'. Rejecting on '1' rejects normal tokens.
- PUBLIC SOLANA RPC METHOD-THROTTLES THE EXACT CALL YOU NEED. api.mainnet-beta.solana.com returned 429 'Too many requests for a specific RPC call' on getTokenLargestAccounts while getTokenSupply and getAccountInfo succeeded in the same second. Concentration analysis is not viable on the public RPC. Budget a paid provider.
- getTokenLargestAccounts RETURNS TOKEN ACCOUNTS, NOT OWNERS, AND CAPS AT 20. One owner can hold many ATAs, so summing the 20 rows without resolving and grouping by .parsed.info.owner both understates single-entity concentration and overstates holder count. You need a follow-up getMultipleAccounts on all 20.
- SCORE POLARITY IS INCONSISTENT ACROSS VENDORS. RugCheck, Solana Tracker and Honeypot.is are higher = riskier. TokenSniffer and SolSniffer are higher = safer. Mixing them without normalising at the adapter boundary inverts your gate for those two providers. Pin the polarity with a unit test against USDC and a known rug per vendor.
- JUPITER-VERIFIED TOKENS GET A FREE PASS FROM SOLANA TRACKER. Their docs state Jupiter-verified tokens are automatically assigned risk score 0 with no risk factors. That is a whitelist, not an analysis. Never use their score as your sole gate.
- HONEYPOT.IS OMITS riskLevel WHEN risk == 'unknown'. Code that defaults a missing numeric to 0 will read 'we could not analyse this' as 'perfectly safe'. Treat absent as reject.
- A CLEAN HONEYPOT SIMULATION IS NOT SUFFICIENT WHEN slippage_modifiable == '1'. GoPlus exposes slippage_modifiable and personal_slippage_modifiable — the owner can raise a currently-0% sell tax to 100% after your buy lands, and per-address taxes mean the simulation address may be treated differently from yours. Always AND the simulation result with these flags.
- mintCloseAuthority INVALIDATES CACHING. A mint carrying this extension can be closed and re-initialised at the same address with a different extension set. Any safety verdict you cached for that mint is void. Reject on its presence, or re-verify immediately before every transaction.
- TRENCH.BOT WAS UNREACHABLE FROM MY EGRESS. The TLS handshake completed and then the request hung to a 60-second timeout (Cloudflare or geo gating). The endpoint URL and response schema are confirmed from an OpenAPI spec and multiple production callers, but verify reachability from your own production egress before building a dependency, and never let a bundle check block the hot path.
- SOLSNIFFER'S PUBLIC DOCS ARE INACCESSIBLE. solsniffer.com/api-v2/docs/ returned HTTP 403 unauthenticated and their public GitHub README is marketing copy with no endpoints, no schema, no indicator list, and no pricing. Do not architect around it sight-unseen. Same caveat, weaker, for De.Fi Scanner — I could not retrieve a concrete endpoint URL or response schema, so treat that entire row as unverified.
- RUGCHECK'S DOCUMENTED VS ACTUAL RATE LIMIT DISAGREE. FluxRPC documents '1 per second' for the public endpoint; the server returns x-rate-limit-limit: 15 and served 18 consecutive requests without a 429. Do not tune to either extreme — 10/s with backoff on 429 is the defensible design.
- RUGCHECK /v1/stats/trending RETURNED null IN MY SAMPLE, not an empty array. Any consumer doing `for (t of response)` crashes. All the stats endpoints need null guards.
- BURN AND LOCK ARE NOT THE SAME THING. Burned LP is irreversible. Locked LP has an endTime. GoPlus's locker endpoint returns endTime per lock; a lock expiring inside your holding horizon offers zero protection but will register as 'locked' in every boolean field. Compare endTime against now + max_hold.
- THE FUNDING-GRAPH HEURISTIC CLUSTERS INNOCENT RETAIL IF YOU DO NOT EXCLUDE EXCHANGES. Every user who withdrew from the same exchange hot wallet shares a funder. You must maintain an exchange-address denylist. Note the asymmetry: a shared HOT WALLET funder is noise, but a holder sending TO a CEX deposit address (many-in / one-out to a known hot wallet) is a real exit signal.
- SINGLE-HOP FUNDING ANALYSIS MISSES MOST OBFUSCATION. Pine Analytics explicitly caveat that their 15,000-token figure captures only direct-funding links and misses multi-hop strategies. Depth-1 tracing will systematically under-detect. Walk at least 2 hops.
- BASE RATES MAKE NAIVE PRECISION CLAIMS MEANINGLESS. MemeTrans labelled 84.13% of 41,470 migrated pump.fun tokens as high-risk and only 4.53% as low-risk. A filter that rejects everything scores 84% 'accuracy'. Evaluate on AUPRC and on realised PnL of the accepted set, never on accuracy. Their best model reached AUPRC 0.5729 and cut losses from -60.71% to -26.64% — the safety layer reduces bleed, it does not manufacture edge.
- VENDOR HOLDER COUNTS AND CONCENTRATION FIGURES ARE NOT CROSS-COMPARABLE. GoPlus returns 10 top holders, RugCheck 20; GoPlus holder counts run ~3% below RugCheck's. Averaging or cross-validating a numeric concentration field across providers produces a number that means nothing. Pick one source per numeric field; only the boolean authority fields are safe to cross-check (and there the two do agree).
- RUGCHECK'S PER-HOLDER insider BOOLEAN DOES NOT MATCH graphInsidersDetected. An independent integrator documented that these do not reconcile 1:1. Do not compute insider supply by summing topHolders where insider == true; use the insider graph nodes instead.
- SAFETY VERDICTS DECAY IN SECONDS ON A FRESH LAUNCH. Authorities can be revoked, LP can be pulled, and metadata can be mutated between your check and your fill. Any cache TTL longer than a few seconds on a sub-1-hour-old token is a correctness bug, not an optimisation. Re-verify the tier-0 native checks immediately before signing.
- DO NOT PUT A THIRD-PARTY API IN THE HOT PATH. RugCheck at 10/s, Birdeye at 1,200 free calls/month, Honeypot.is with undisclosed limits, and Trench possibly unreachable are all unsuitable as blocking dependencies for a latency-sensitive entry. Architect as: tier-0 native RPC hard gate (never blocks on a vendor) → probe fill → tier-1 async vendor enrichment that can veto the size-up but not the probe.

### Each dangerous Token-2022 extension maps to a distinct concrete attack, and each is independently detectable from mint account data.

transferHook: arbitrary program runs on every transfer and can revert — a perfect honeypot (buys allowed, sells reverted); detect via extensions[].extension=='transferHook', inspect state.programId and reject unless allowlisted. permanentDelegate: the delegate can transfer or burn any holder's balance with no signature — in 2026 used for automated post-buy theft; detect via extension=='permanentDelegate', state.delegate. nonTransferable: soulbound, can never be sold. defaultAccountState with state 'frozen': the mint looks normal on DEX listings, the buy succeeds, and the buyer's fresh token account is created frozen so the sell fails. confidentialTransferMint: hides transfer amounts, so whale dumps are invisible to your own volume/holder analytics. mintCloseAuthority: the mint can be closed and re-initialised at the same address with a DIFFERENT extension set — a clean mint can become a hooked one without changing address. transferFeeConfig: fee is deducted from the RECIPIENT, so your received amount is less than quoted and your PnL math silently breaks.

**Actionable:** Encode as a hard denylist keyed on extension name. For mintCloseAuthority specifically, presence alone is a reject for a trading bot because it invalidates any cached safety verdict — you cannot assume a mint you cleared 10 minutes ago still has the same extensions.

### Same-slot / same-block buying is the primary bundle signature on Solana, and the operative window is one 400ms slot.

### Raydium LP burn percentage has an exact formula that handles the case where the pool reserve exceeds current supply.

### The funding-graph / common-funder heuristic is the highest-signal clustering method and Bubblemaps describes the exact pattern it keys on.

### Academic A-A wash-trade definitions give you two precise DEX-specific rules beyond the same-address case.

An A-A wash trade (self-trade) is any trade where the buyer address equals the seller address. On an AMM the DEX-specific form is: the swapper's address is identical to one of the pool's liquidity providers — the LP is trading against their own pool, which costs them only the fee and manufactures volume. Multi-party form: the LP address and the swapper addresses differ but are controlled by one entity — e.g. 0x1 is the dominant LP while 0x2, 0x3, 0x4 pose as distinct swappers. Round-trip detection: flag when buyer and seller identities are inverted in sequential transactions. The NFT literature's simplest usable rule is flagging a repurchase of the same asset by the same wallet within 30 days.

**Actionable:** Cheapest high-value check for a memecoin: intersect the set of addresses that provided liquidity with the set of addresses that swapped in the first hour. Any overlap is an A-A wash trade by definition and requires no clustering, no ML, and one pass over the trade log. For Solana pump.fun this maps to: is the bonding-curve creator also among the early buyers. Second: compute the ratio unique_traders / total_trades — an organic launch trends toward many traders and few trades each; a wash-traded one has few traders and many trades. Reject when trades_per_unique_trader exceeds your bar.
